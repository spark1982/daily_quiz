The topic

Topic

Explain how to take good care of a pet.

Type of writing

Informative — Teach the reader about something, step by step.

How to write it

  1. Introduce the topic: name the pet (a dog, a cat, a fish, or another pet you know) and say you will explain how to take care of it.
  2. Explain the jobs in order: First, Next, Then, Finally (for example: food and water, a clean home, exercise or play, a visit to the vet).
  3. For one job, tell why it matters for the pet.
  4. End with a sentence that tells what happens when a pet is well cared for.

Sentence starter

“Taking good care of a pet is a big job, but it is easy if you follow a few important steps.” (You can use this or write your own first sentence.)

Try to use these words

next  ·  healthy  ·  gentle

Task: Write one paragraph (60–120 words). Use the plan above and try to use the three words.

Your writing

Taking care of a pet is a big job, but it is easy if you follow a few important steps. I will show you how to take care of a dog. First, you must give it food and water. What you did great

  • Your order words are perfect. “First, you must give it food and water. Next, you have to walk it every day to keep it healthy. Then, you must take it to the vet” — the reader always knows which step comes next.
  • You told why a job matters. “Walk it every day to keep it healthy” and “if you are not gentle, it might get angry and bite you” both give the reason behind the job. That is exactly what the plan asked for.

Let’s make it even better

Here is everything that will make your paragraph stronger, starting with the most important. (Grammar is in its own section below.)

  1. 1. Make Finally the last step. Finally tells the reader “this is the last one.” But after your Finally sentence, you added another job (being gentle). Move the gentle step before Finally so the steps stay in order.

    Before: “Finally, make sure it has a loved home… You also have to be gentle…”
    After: “While you do all of these jobs, always be gentle… Finally, make sure it has a loving home…”

  2. 2. End with what happens when a pet is well cared for. The plan asked for an ending that tells the good result. Your last sentence tells what happens if you do not take care of the pet, and it says you would “get another one,” which makes a pet sound easy to replace. A happy, clear ending leaves the reader with your main idea. I rewrote it.

    Before: “You have to take care of it because then it might not survive and you will have to get another one or not get any pet.”
    After: “When you take good care of your dog, it will stay healthy and happy and be your best friend for many years.”

  3. 3. Say exactly when to go to the vet. “When needed” is vague — the reader may not know when that is. Teaching writing gives exact details.

    Before: “Then, you must take it to the vet when needed.”
    After: “Then, you must take it to the vet for a checkup every year and whenever it seems sick.”

  4. 4. Add a small detail to the food step. A few exact words make your teaching clearer: what kind of water, and how often?

    Before: “First, you must give it food and water.”
    After: “First, you must give it fresh food and water every day.”

  5. 5. Choose the exact word: loving, not loved. A home that gives love is a loving home. Also, “it is happy for as much of the time as possible” is long and wordy. Say it shorter.

    Before: “Finally, make sure it has a loved home and it is happy for as much of the time as possible.”
    After: “Finally, make sure it has a loving home where it feels safe and happy.”

  6. 6. Say your dog instead of it sometimes. Your paragraph uses it many times. Saying your dog once in a while reminds the reader what you are talking about. Also, dogs usually bite when they are scared, so that is the more exact word.

    Before: “if you are not gentle, it might get angry and bite you.”
    After: “if you are not gentle, your dog might get scared and bite you.”
