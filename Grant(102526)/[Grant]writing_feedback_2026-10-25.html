The topic

Topic

Write about the most exciting day you remember.

Type of writing

Narrative — Tell a story with a beginning, middle, and end.

How to write it

  1. Beginning: tell who is in the story and where it happens.
  2. Middle: tell what happens, in order. Use time words like first, then, and after that.
  3. Add one detail that shows how a character feels (what they say, do, or think).
  4. End: tell how the story ends and how you felt.

Sentence starter

“I will never forget the day when ...” (You can use this or write your own first sentence.)

Try to use these words

suddenly  ·  nervous  ·  proud

Task: Write one paragraph (60–120 words). Use the plan above and try to use the three words.

What you did great

  • A strong beginning. “I will never forget the day when I had my tenth birthday party.” The reader knows right away who, what, and why this day was special. And “one of the greatest moments of my life” makes us want to hear more.
  • Clear time order. You used “First,” “Next,” “After that,” and “Then,” with commas after each one. The reader can follow the whole party step by step.

Let’s make it even better

Here is everything that will make your story stronger, starting with the most important. (Grammar is in its own section below.)

  1. 1. Show a feeling in the middle of the story. The plan asked for one detail that shows how a character feels (what they say, do, or think). Right now the story tells what you did, but not how it felt while it happened. Bowling is a great spot for this, and it is the perfect place for all three of your words! I added two example sentences — change them to what really happened.

    Before: “Next, we went bowling and it was very fun.”
    After: “Next, we went bowling, and it was a blast. When it was my turn, I felt nervous because all my friends were watching. Suddenly, my ball crashed into the pins and knocked them all down, and I felt so proud when my friends cheered.”

  2. 2. Name things exactly. “A safe fake gun game” takes many words to describe something that has a short name. If it was laser tag, just say laser tag! Also, “played games and played” repeats played.

    Before: “so we played games and played a safe fake gun game.”
    After: “so we played arcade games and laser tag.”

  3. 3. Choose words that are true and clear. “Infinite” means it never ends — but the party did end. You probably mean you could play as much as you wanted. Say that.

    Before: “First, we had infinite time,”
    After: “First, we had an unlimited game pass,”

  4. 4. Replace “very fun” and “lots of food” with exact words. “Very fun” and “lots of food” do not help the reader picture anything. Name the food, or use a livelier word like a blast.

    Before: “After that, we ate pizza and lots of food.”
    After: “After that, we ate pizza and birthday cake until we were full.”

  5. 5. Use Finally for the last event, and end with your feeling. Finally tells the reader the story is ending. “Went home and I went back home” repeats home, and “feeling so good” is a little plain. End by telling how you felt about the whole day.

    Before: “Then, my friends went home and I went back home feeling so good after the party.”
    After: “Finally, my friends went home, and I went to bed smiling because it was the best birthday I ever had.”

  6. 6. Put the who and where together at the start. Your first two sentences tell who and where. Joining the place into the first sentence makes a smooth beginning, and the next sentence can tell how great it was.

    Before: “…my tenth birthday party. The party with my friends was at Main Event, and it was one of the greatest moments of my life.”
    After: “…my tenth birthday party with my friends at Main Event. It was one of the greatest days of my life.”

Grammar fixes

  1. 1. Use a comma before and when it joins two complete sentences. “We went bowling” and “it was very fun” could each be a sentence by itself. When and joins two of them, put a comma before and.

    Before: “Next, we went bowling and it was very fun.”
    After: “Next, we went bowling, and it was a blast.”

  2. 2. Same rule in your last sentence. “My friends went home” and “I went back home” are both complete sentences, so they need a comma before and.

    Before: “Then, my friends went home and I went back home…”
    After: “Finally, my friends went home, and I went to bed smiling…”

Everything else is spelled correctly, and you used a comma after every time word — nice!

Did you use the three words?

  • suddenly — No — this one was missing. It is perfect for a surprise moment: “Suddenly, my ball crashed into the pins.”
  • nervous — No — this one was missing. Use it to show a feeling before something happens: “I felt nervous because all my friends were watching.”
  • proud — No — this one was missing. Use it after you do something well: “I felt so proud when my friends cheered.”
