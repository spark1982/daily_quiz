The topic

Topic

What is the best superpower to have?

Type of writing

Opinion — Tell what you think and give reasons.

How to write it

  1. Start by telling your opinion: which superpower is the best one to have?
  2. Give your first reason and an example (something the power would let you do, like helping people or getting somewhere fast).
  3. Give your second reason and an example.
  4. End with a closing sentence that says your opinion again in a new way.

Sentence starter

“I think the best superpower to have is the power to fly, because I could go anywhere I wanted.” (You can use this or write your own first sentence.)

Try to use these words

in addition  ·  incredible  ·  useful

Task: Write one paragraph (60–120 words). Use the plan above and try to use the three words.

What you did great

  • A clear opinion right away. “I think the best superpower to have is the power to fly” tells the reader exactly what you believe in the first sentence — that is how a strong opinion paragraph starts.
  • A real, specific example. “If they forgot their keys, I could get them in about 10 seconds” is a great example: it is small, believable, and shows how flying helps someone. The number “10 seconds” makes it even stronger.

Let’s make it even better

Here is everything that will make your paragraph stronger, starting with the most important. (Grammar is in its own section below.)

  1. 1. Give two different reasons. The plan asked for a first reason and a second reason. Your paragraph says “I could go anywhere I wanted” three times (in your first sentence, after “Also,” and after “In addition,”). Saying the same reason again does not make it stronger — the reader wants something new. You already have two good reasons hiding in your paragraph: flying lets you help people, and flying is fun. Use one for “First” and the other for “In addition.”

    Before: “Also, I could go anywhere I wanted like the playground. In addition, I basically could go anywhere I wanted.”
    After: “In addition, flying would be a lot of fun, because I could zoom to places like the playground across town in seconds.”

  2. 2. Add an example for your second reason. Your first reason has a great example (the keys). Your second reason needs one too, so the reader can picture the fun.

    Before: (no example for the fun reason)
    After: “I could even look down at my whole neighborhood from high above the trees.”

  3. 3. Split the example into its own sentence. “I could help someone like if they forgot their keys, I could get them” squeezes the reason and the example into one tangled sentence. Say the reason first, then start the example with For example,.

    Before: “First, I could help someone like if they forgot their keys, I could get them in about 10 seconds.”
    After: “First, I could help people quickly. For example, if a friend forgot their keys, I could fly home and get them in about 10 seconds.”

  4. 4. Say your opinion again in a new way at the end. Your closing sentence almost repeats your first sentence. A strong closing sums up both reasons. This is also a perfect spot for your word incredible.

    Before: “I think flying is the best superpower because then you can fly around in the sky and have fun.”
    After: “That is why flying is the most incredible superpower: it lets me help others and have fun in the sky.”

  5. 5. Drop filler words. Basically and pretty don’t add meaning. “Pretty useful” sounds unsure — in an opinion paragraph you want to sound sure.

    Before: “which is pretty useful”
    After: “which would be really useful”

Grammar fixes

  1. 1. Put a comma before which when it adds extra information. “Which is pretty useful” describes the whole idea before it, so it needs a comma in front.

    Before: “I could go anywhere I wanted which is pretty useful.”
    After: “I could go anywhere I wanted, which would be really useful.”

  2. 2. Keep the same tense for something imaginary. You don’t really have this power, so use would and could all the way through. “Which is useful” switches to the real present.

    Before: “which is pretty useful”
    After: “which would be really useful”

  3. 3. Don’t use like if. Like and if together sound like talking, not writing. Use For example, if or just if.

    Before: “like if they forgot their keys”
    After: “For example, if a friend forgot their keys”

  4. 4. Don’t switch from I to you. The whole paragraph is about what I could do, but the last sentence says “then you can fly around.” Stay with I (or me).

    Before: “because then you can fly around in the sky and have fun.”
    After: “it lets me help others and have fun in the sky.”

  5. 5. Use a comma before an example that starts with like. When like means “for example,” put a comma before it.

    Before: “anywhere I wanted like the playground”
    After: “to places like the playground” (or “anywhere I wanted, like the playground”)

All of your words are spelled correctly, and you used a comma after “First,” “Also,” and “In addition,” — nice!

Did you use the three words?

  • in addition — Yes! “In addition, I basically could go anywhere I wanted.” Next time, follow it with a new reason.
  • incredible — Not yet. It fits perfectly in your closing: “flying is the most incredible superpower.”
  • useful — Yes! “…which is pretty useful.”
