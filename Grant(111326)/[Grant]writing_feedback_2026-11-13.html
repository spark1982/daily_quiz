The topic

Topic

Explain what a normal school day is like for someone who has never been to your school.

Type of writing

Informative — Teach the reader about something, step by step.

How to write it

  1. Start by telling the reader what your school is and who it is for.
  2. Explain the day in order. Use First, Next, Then, and Finally.
  3. Tell about one special part of the day, like lunch or recess.
  4. End with a closing sentence about why you like your school day.

Try to use these words

schedule  ·  finally  ·  favorite

Task: Write one paragraph (60–120 words). Use the plan above and try to use the three words.

Your writing

If you don’t know what a school day looks like, you will learn what it is like now. My school is called Ravenwood Elementary, and it is for people who want to learn and have fun. What you did great

  • You used real, exact details. Times like “morning work at 8:55” and “ELA at 10:15” make your writing feel true. A new student would know exactly what to expect.
  • You told about a special part of the day. “recess is my favorite, because we get to play outside” answers step 3 of the plan and shows the reader how you feel.

Let’s make it even better

Here is everything that will make your paragraph stronger, starting with the most important. (Grammar is in its own section below.)

  1. 1. Save Finally for the very last step. Finally tells the reader “this is the end.” You used it at 2:30, but then more things happened (snack, read aloud, science, going home). Move Finally to the real end of the day, and use words like At 2:30 or After that for the middle steps.

    Before: “Finally, we go out at recess at 2:30, then when we come back…”
    After: “At 2:30, we have recess again…” … “Finally, at 3:45, we pack up and go home.”

  2. 2. Add a closing sentence. Step 4 of the plan asked you to end with why you like your school day. Your paragraph stops at going home. A closing sentence wraps up your writing — and it is a perfect place to use your helper word schedule.

    Before: (no closing sentence)
    After: “I like my school day because it has a clear schedule with plenty of time to learn and play.”

  3. 3. Break long “and then” chains into shorter sentences. One sentence had three parts joined by and then. Short sentences are easier for a new reader to follow. Give each important time its own sentence, or list the times together.

    Before: “First, we usually do morning work at 8:55, and then we do band or strings at 9;00 or 9:30, and then we go out at recess at 10:00.”
    After: “First, we do morning work at 8:55, and then we go to band or strings. At 10:00, we go out to recess.”

  4. 4. Don’t say the same idea twice. Your first sentence already says the reader “will learn what it is like now”, so “And you will learn the schedule for our school” repeats it. I removed that sentence. Your opening also works better if it speaks to the new student directly.

    Before: “If you don’t know what a school day looks like, you will learn what it is like now.” “And you will learn the schedule for our school.”
    After: “If you have never been to my school, here is what a normal day is like.” (second sentence removed)

  5. 5. Keep it to one paragraph-sized length. Your paragraph was 179 words — the goal was 60–120. Lots of detail is great, but you can combine small steps. For example, put the three morning classes in one list, and put the two math parts together.

    Before: “Then, we go back to class to do math.” “We do math from 1:00 to 2:00 and then from 2:00 to 2:30, we do a math assignment and turn it in.”
    After: “Then, we go back to class for math from 1:00 to 2:30, and we finish with a math assignment that we turn in.”

  6. 6. Use exact, natural words. “the teacher does read aloud” sounds unusual. Use the action word by itself: reads aloud. Also, while shows that two things happen at the same time.

    Before: “we get to eat our snack, and the teacher does read aloud.”
    After: “we eat our snack while the teacher reads aloud.”

Grammar fixes

  1. 1. Times use a colon (:), not a semicolon (;). Between the hour and the minutes, always write a colon.

    Before: “9;00”
    After: “9:00”

  2. 2. Start every sentence with a capital letter. Your sentence about recess began with a small letter, so it looked like part of the sentence before it.

    Before: “…the teacher does read aloud. recess is my favorite…”
    After: “…the teacher reads aloud. Recess is my favorite…”

  3. 3. Use a period or and between two full sentences, not just a comma. “then we go to specials” and “then we go to lunch” are each complete sentences, so a comma alone cannot join them (that is called a run-on). You can make a list instead.

    Before: “Next, we do ELA at 10:15, then we go to specials at 11:15, then we go to lunch at 12:15.”
    After: “Next, we have ELA at 10:15, specials at 11:15, and lunch at 12:15.”

  4. 4. We go out to recess. Use to when you move toward a place or an activity.

    Before: “we go out at recess at 10:00”
    After: “At 10:00, we go out to recess.”

  5. 5. No comma before because here. When the because part comes at the end of a sentence, you usually don’t need a comma before it.

    Before: “recess is my favorite, because we get to play outside”
    After: “Recess is my favorite because we get to play outside.”

  6. 6. Try not to start a sentence with And. In school writing, join the idea to the sentence before it, or start a different way.

    Before: “And you will learn the schedule for our school.”
    After: (removed — see #4 above)

Your writing, polished

If you have never been to my school, here is what a normal day is like. My school is called Ravenwood Elementary, and it is a place where kids learn and have fun. First, we do morning work at 8:55, and then we go to band or strings. At 10:00, we go out to recess. Next, we have ELA at 10:15, specials at 11:15, and lunch at 12:15. When we come back, we eat our snack while the teacher reads aloud. After that, we do science or social studies. Finally, at 3:45, we pack up and go home. I like my school day because it has a clear schedule with plenty of time to learn and play.
