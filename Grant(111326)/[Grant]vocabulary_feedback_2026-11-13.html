You have the right idea: restrict is about not letting something happen. But your sentence stops before it says what is not allowed (“to not let something to something”). Finish the thought: “to limit something, so it can only go so far.” Example: a sign can restrict parking to one hour.

conserve (verb) Not yet

Your answer

to get something or make a reservation

Meaning: to save something and use it carefully so it does not run out; to protect it

Example: We turn off the lights when we leave a room to conserve energy.

This one is easy to mix up! Conserve sounds like reserve (to save a seat or make a reservation), but it means the opposite of using things up. When you conserve water or energy, you save it by using less. Think of conservation: protecting forests and animals.

evident (adjective) Not yet

Your answer

To give evidence

Meaning: easy to see or understand; clear

Example: It was evident that the dog had been in the garden, because there were muddy paw prints everywhere.

Good thinking — evident is related to evidence! But evident is an adjective (a describing word), not an action. It describes something that is clear or easy to see because the evidence is right there. Try: “clear; easy to see.”

Words to practice

  • restrict — limit something
  • conserve — save it and use it carefully
  • evident — clear; easy to see

Memory tip: Conserve and conservation both mean saving — picture a sign that says “Conserve water: turn off the tap!” And when something is evident, the evidence makes it clear.
