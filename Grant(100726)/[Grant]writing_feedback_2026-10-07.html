The topic

Topic

Describe the job of someone you admire.

Type of writing

Informative — Teach the reader about something, step by step.

How to write it

  1. Say who the person is and what their job is.
  2. Explain what they do at work, in order: First, Next, Then, Finally.
  3. Tell one way their job helps other people.
  4. End with a sentence about why you admire them.

Sentence starter

“Someone I admire is ___, who works as a ___.” (You can use this or write your own first sentence.)

Try to use these words

first  ·  responsible  ·  patient

Task: Write one paragraph (60–120 words). Use the plan above and try to use the three words.

Your writing

Someone I admire is my dad, who works as a DoorDash deliverer. First, he picks up the food from the place the customer ordered from. Next, he drives to the house with the person that ordered the food. Then, he delivers it to the customer. Finally, he goes to the next customer and repeats it over again. What you did great

  • Your steps are in perfect order. “First, he picks up the food… Next, he drives… Then, he delivers it… Finally, he goes to the next customer.” A reader who knows nothing about delivery jobs can follow every step. That is exactly what informative writing should do. You even put a comma after each order word!
  • You explained why the job helps people. “One way his job helps people is that they… can save time” answers the third step of the plan, and your last sentence tells why you admire your dad. Your paragraph is 92 words, right in the 60–120 range.

Let’s make it even better

Here is everything that will make your paragraph stronger, starting with the most important. (Grammar mistakes are in their own section below.)

  1. 1. Make the “Next” step say what you mean. “He drives to the house with the person that ordered the food” sounds like the customer is riding in the car with your dad! Put the words in an order that shows he is driving the food to the person’s house.

    Before: “Next, he drives to the house with the person that ordered the food.”
    After: “Next, he drives the food to the house of the person who ordered it.”

  2. 2. Tell why YOU admire him with details only you know. Your ending, “he works for other people and helps them,” is true, but it could be about anyone. Add one detail that shows what kind of person your dad is at work. This is also the perfect place for the helper words responsible and patient.

    Before: “I admire my dad because he works for other people and helps them.”
    After: “I admire my dad because he is responsible and patient. He makes sure every order arrives hot, even when traffic is slow.”

  3. 3. Add one detail to a step. Each step is only a few words long, so the reader cannot picture it. Adding one small detail, like what your dad checks or uses, makes the job come alive.

    Before: “First, he picks up the food from the place the customer ordered from.”
    After: “First, he picks up the food from the restaurant and checks that the order is complete.”

  4. 4. Fix the reason so it makes sense. People do not drive their car to order food; they order on a phone. The time they save is the trip to pick it up.

    Before: “they don’t have to drive their car to order food and can save time.”
    After: “they don’t have to drive to the restaurant to pick up their food, so they save time.”

  5. 5. Don’t say the same thing twice. Repeat already means “do it again,” so “repeats it over again” says “again” two times.

    Before: “he goes to the next customer and repeats it over again.”
    After: “he goes to the next order and repeats the steps.”

  6. 6. Use different words instead of repeating “customer.” The word customer appears three times in a row. Switching words keeps your writing interesting.

    Before: “Then, he delivers it to the customer. Finally, he goes to the next customer”
    After: “Then, he hands the food to the customer. Finally, he goes to the next order”

  7. 7. Use the common name for the job. “Deliverer” is a real word, but people usually say “delivery driver.” Using the word readers expect makes your first sentence smoother.

    Before: “who works as a DoorDash deliverer.”
    After: “who works as a delivery driver for DoorDash.”

Grammar fixes

  1. 1. Use “who” for people. When you talk about a person, use who, not that. Save that for things.

    Before: “the person that ordered the food”
    After: “the person who ordered the food”

That is the only grammar mistake! Your capital letters, commas after order words, and end marks are all correct. Great job!

Did you use the three words?

  • first — Yes! “First, he picks up the food…”
  • responsible — Not yet. Try it in your ending: “He is responsible because he makes sure every order is correct.”
  • patient — Not yet. Try: “He stays patient when he is stuck in traffic.”
