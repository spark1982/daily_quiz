The topic

Topic

Is reading a book better than watching the movie?

Type of writing

Opinion — Tell what you think and give reasons.

How to write it

  1. Start by telling your opinion: is the book better, or is the movie better?
  2. Give your first reason and an example (maybe a book and movie you know, like a story you read and then watched).
  3. Give your second reason and an example.
  4. End with a closing sentence that says your opinion again in a new way.

Sentence starter

“I think reading the book is better than watching the movie because a book lets me imagine everything in my own way.” (You can use this or write your own first sentence.)

however  ·  imagine  ·  detail

What you did great

  • You thought for yourself. The sentence starter said the book was better, but you wrote, “I think watching a movie is better”. A good opinion writer picks the side he really believes — and you did.
  • You gave a real example. “For example, a Harry Potter book” uses a book and movie you actually know. Real examples make an opinion much more convincing than general ideas.

Let’s make it even better

Here is everything that will make your paragraph stronger, starting with the most important. (Grammar mistakes are in their own section below.)

  1. 1. Compare the two things in your first sentence. The topic asks which is better, a book or a movie. Your first sentence says the movie is “better” but never says better than what. Name both sides, and tell your main reason clearly. “it lets me watch something and what happens” is a little hard to follow.

    Before: “I think watching a movie is better because it lets me watch something and what happens.”
    After: “I think watching a movie is better than reading the book because a movie lets me see exactly what happens.”

  2. 2. Don’t repeat “what is happening”. You wrote “see what is actually happening” and “you can actually see what is happening” — the same words twice, with actually twice too. Instead, show the reader what details a movie gives you. Specific details are stronger than repeating a phrase.

    Before: “It also has more detail in the movie so you can actually see what is happening.”
    After: “A movie also shows more detail, like the costumes, the castles, and the magic spells.”

  3. 3. Say something nice about the other side in its own short sentence. Good opinion writers admit the other side has a good point, then explain why their side still wins. Saying it in a short sentence first, and then starting the next one with However, makes this very clear. Also, “watch the story come to life” paints a better picture than “see what is actually happening”.

    Before: “However, the book is although good, but the movie is better because then you can sit and relax while still being able to see what is actually happening.”
    After: “The book is good, too. However, the movie is better because I can sit back and relax while I watch the story come to life.”

  4. 4. Make your closing sentence sound like an ending. Start with words that tell the reader you are finishing, like “That is why”, and say your opinion again in a new way.

    Before: “Movies are better because then you can see the picture in the movie than in your brain.”
    After: “That is why I think movies are better: I can see the pictures on the screen instead of only in my brain.”

Grammar fixes

  1. 1. Finish the example phrase. “For example, a Harry Potter book,” is missing words, so the sentence doesn’t connect. Add when I read so it makes sense.

    Before: “For example, a Harry Potter book, I can’t imagine the picture in the book as well as I can in the movie.”
    After: “For example, when I read a Harry Potter book, I can’t imagine the pictures as well as I can see them in the movie.”

  2. 2. Use only one “turning” word. However, although, and but all mean “the other side.” Use just one at a time. (Also, although goes at the start of a part of the sentence, never in the middle like “is although good”.)

    Before: “However, the book is although good, but the movie is better”
    After: “The book is good, too. However, the movie is better”

  3. 3. “Than” needs a comparing word. Use than after words like better or more. In “see the picture in the movie than in your brain”, there is no comparing word, so use instead of.

    Before: “you can see the picture in the movie than in your brain”
    After: “I can see the pictures on the screen instead of only in my brain”

  4. 4. Stay with “I.” You start with “I think” and “lets me”, then switch to “you can sit and relax”. In your own opinion paragraph, keep using I and me so the voice stays the same.

    Before: “then you can sit and relax”
    After: “I can sit back and relax”
