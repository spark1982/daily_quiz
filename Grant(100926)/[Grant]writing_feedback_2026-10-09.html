The topic

Topic

Your pet can suddenly talk for one day.

Type of writing

Narrative — Tell a story with a beginning, middle, and end.

How to write it

  1. Beginning: Tell who your pet is and how you find out it can talk.
  2. Middle: Tell one or two funny or surprising things your pet says or does, in order.
  3. Add details: What does its voice sound like? How do you and your family react?
  4. End: Tell how the day ends and how you feel about it.

Sentence starter

“When I poured breakfast into my pet’s bowl, a tiny voice said, ‘Not this again!’” (You can use this or write your own first sentence.)

Your writing

What you did great

  • Your funny details make the dog come alive. “He had a very high voice and sounded like a girl, but actually was a boy” is a surprise the reader did not expect, and “He also said, ‘Let’s watch TV!’” is exactly what a dog might ask for. The plan asked what the voice sounds like, and you answered it.
  • Your story moves through the whole day. It starts at breakfast, goes on a walk, moves to the couch, and ends at bedtime: “Then when i got tired, i went to bed.” That is a clear beginning, middle, and end, and your paragraph is 120 words, right at the top of the 60–120 range.

Let’s make it even better

Here is everything that will make your story stronger, starting with the most important. (Grammar mistakes are in their own section below.)

  1. 1. Don’t say the starter line twice. Your first sentence already has the dog say “Not this again!” Then the second sentence says it again. Use the second sentence to tell the reader something new: who your pet is.

    Before: “I had a dog, and i realized it could talk when it said, “Not this again!””
    After: “I have a dog, and that is how I realized he could talk.”

  2. 2. Use “have” for a pet you still own. “I had a dog” sounds like you don’t have the dog anymore. Since the dog is part of your life now, say “have.”

    Before: “I had a dog”
    After: “I have a dog”

  3. 3. Say who was confused. “While the others were confused” makes the reader stop and wonder: which others? Say that the people he greeted were the confused ones.

    Before: “he started to greet other people while the others were confused.”
    After: “he started to greet other people, who looked very confused.”

  4. 4. Tell who you were talking to. “I laughed and said he could talk” does not say who heard you. Adding two words makes the scene clear.

    Before: “I laughed and said he could talk.”
    After: “I laughed and told them he could talk.”

  5. 5. Pick the exact word. “Sneaked” means moved quietly so no one would notice, so “he sneaked it perfectly” does not make sense here. Also, “you need special training” is confusing, because no training can make a dog talk. Say what really surprised your family.

    Before: “because you need special training to do something like that, but he sneaked it perfectly.”
    After: “because dogs are not supposed to talk, but he spoke perfectly.”

  6. 6. End with a stronger feeling. “Feeling nice” is a quiet ending for such an amazing day. Choose a feeling word that matches a talking dog! You could also tell the reader if the dog stopped talking.

    Before: “i went to bed feeling nice.”
    After: “I went to bed feeling happy and amazed, wondering if he would still talk tomorrow.”

  7. 7. Show the funny part with a detail. “He started to greet other people” is a great funny idea. One detail of what he said would make readers laugh even more.

    Before: “he started to greet other people”
    After: “he started to say ‘Good morning!’ to everyone we passed”

Grammar fixes

  1. 1. Always capitalize “I.” When you write about yourself, the word I is always a capital letter, even in the middle of a sentence. It happened three times.

    Before: “and i realized… Then when i got tired, i went to bed”
    After: “and I realized… Then when I got tired, I went to bed”

  2. 2. Call your dog “he” the whole time. You called the dog “it” first and then “him” and “he.” Pick one and keep it. Since he is a boy dog, use he and him.

    Before: “i realized it could talk when it said”
    After: “I realized he could talk when he said”

  3. 3. Every part of a sentence needs a subject. In “but actually was a boy,” the reader has to guess who was a boy. Add he.

    Before: “sounded like a girl, but actually was a boy.”
    After: “sounded like a girl, but he was actually a boy.”

  4. 4. Use double quotation marks for talking. When a character speaks in your story, put the words inside double marks (“ ”). The sentence starter used single marks (‘ ’) only because it was already inside another quotation.

    Before: “a tiny voice said, ‘Not this again!’”
    After: “a tiny voice said, “Not this again!””

  5. 5. Put a comma after “Then” at the start. When a time word like Then starts a sentence and more words come before the main part, a comma helps the reader pause.

    Before: “Then when i got tired, i went to bed”
    After: “Then, when I got tired, I went to bed”

Your end marks and the exclamation points inside the dog’s words are correct. Fixing the capital I will make the biggest difference!

Did you use the three words?

  • then — Yes! “Then when i got tired, i went to bed…”
  • surprised — Yes! “My family was very surprised…”
  • grumpy — Not yet. The dog’s first words, “Not this again!”, already sound grumpy, so try: “a tiny, grumpy voice said…”
