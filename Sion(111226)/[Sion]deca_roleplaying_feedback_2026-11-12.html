The question

Instructional Area

Operations (Business Administration Core)

Performance Indicator

Explain the nature of risk management.

The judge

The judge plays the owner of a small neighborhood bakery who is thinking about opening a business account at your credit union. The owner asks you: “People keep telling me I need to ‘manage my risks.’ What does that actually mean for a business like mine?”

Task

In one paragraph (100–180 words), explain this performance indicator so the judge can understand it. Define it in plain words, give one specific example that fits the judge’s business, and say why it matters.

Your answer

Essentially, "managing your risks" means assessing, considering, and preparing for the potential risks of a certain decision or situation. In plain English, that means being careful with what could go wrong. That is the nature of risk management. For your case specifically, you need to manage the risks of opening a new business account for your bakery. A business account can certainly be very useful, but you have to consider how to utilize one effectively. Profit is unpredictable: some months might have better or worse sales. However, the payments you need to make for the account don't compensate for that. If a business card starts filling in too many gaps in your budget, the debt you owe could increase exponentially, boomerang back, and demand unaffordable payments at inconvenient times. Managing the risk of this could include setting regulations on the use of the account. For example, you could set a spending limit that you can guarantee you can pay off later even during bad months. Failing to "manage the risk" like this could leave you with much worse problems, like a huge debt bill, later.

What the judge was looking for

  2. The main ways to respond to a risk — avoid, reduce, transfer (insurance, contracts), or accept — with at least one shown in action.
  3. A bakery-specific example and a reason it matters: protecting cash flow and keeping the business open when something goes wrong.

Model answer

Managing your risks simply means planning ahead for the things that could hurt your bakery, so one bad event doesn't sink the business. Every business faces risks it can't control, and risk management is the habit of spotting them, deciding how serious each one is, and choosing what to do: avoid the risk, reduce it, pass it to someone else, or accept it. For example, your sales probably drop every January after the holidays. You can't avoid that, but you can reduce the danger by keeping two months of expenses in a savings account with us and setting up automatic transfers during your busy season. If your main oven breaks, equipment insurance passes that cost to the insurer instead of to you. This matters because a small business with no cushion can be forced to close by a single slow month or repair bill, while a bakery that has planned for those risks can keep its doors open and keep growing.
