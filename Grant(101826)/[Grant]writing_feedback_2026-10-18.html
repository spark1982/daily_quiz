The topic

Topic

You shrink to the size of an ant in your backyard.

Type of writing

Narrative — Tell a story with a beginning, middle, and end.

How to write it

  1. Beginning: tell who you are, where you are, and how you suddenly became tiny.
  2. Middle: tell what happens next — something big (like a giant ant, a raindrop, or a blade of grass) becomes a problem or an adventure.
  3. Tell how you solve the problem or get back to your normal size.
  4. End: tell how the story ends and how you felt.

Sentence starter

“One sunny afternoon, I was lying in the grass when everything around me suddenly started to grow.” (You can use this or write your own first sentence.)

Try to use these words

suddenly  ·  enormous  ·  tiny

What you did great

  • You followed the story plan. You start in the grass, meet a problem (“A drop of water went towards me”), solve it (“the second it touched me, I started growing”), and end at bedtime. That is exactly how a narrative should move.
  • You showed the size change from the ant’s point of view. “They were the size of me” and “Then I was enormous to the ants” are like two bookends: tiny at the start, enormous at the end. Clever!

Let’s make it even better

Here is everything that will make your story stronger, starting with the most important. (Grammar mistakes are in their own section below.)

  1. 1. Tell the reader that YOU are shrinking. Your second sentence says the same thing as your first (“everything around me was growing”). A reader might think the grass is really growing! This is the big surprise of your story, so say it clearly: the world is not getting bigger — you are getting smaller.

    Before: “My name is Grant, and everything around me was growing.”
    After: “My name is Grant, and I quickly realized that the grass was not growing. I was shrinking!”

  2. 2. Make the “tiny” sentence show cause and effect. “I was tiny as I saw many ants” sounds like you became tiny because you saw ants. Use so … that to show that being tiny made the ants look huge.

    Before: “I was tiny as I saw many ants, and they were the size of me.”
    After: “Soon I was so tiny that the ants crawling past me were as big as I was.”

  3. 3. Use exact action verbs instead of “went towards” twice. Ants crawl, and water drops roll or drip. Exact verbs help the reader see the scene, and you don’t repeat yourself.

    Before: “One of them went towards me.” / “A drop of water went towards me”
    After: “One of them crawled toward me.” / “Then a drop of water rolled down a blade of grass toward me”

  4. 4. Pick a better word than “felt.” “It felt me” sounds a little strange. Ants touch and tap things with their antennae to learn about them, so say that.

    Before: “It felt me with its antennas”
    After: “It tapped me with its antennae”

  5. 5. Don’t start two sentences in a row with “Then.” “Then I became my normal size. Then I was enormous to the ants.” Join them into one sentence with a time phrase, and use next to to compare sizes.

    Before: “Then I became my normal size. Then I was enormous to the ants.”
    After: “In a few seconds, I was back to my normal size, and now I was enormous next to the ants.”

  6. 6. Use “wondering if” and add a time word to your ending. When you ask yourself a question in your head, the word is wondering, not thinking. A time word like “That night” also tells the reader the adventure is over.

    Before: “then, I went to sleep thinking if I would be tiny again.”
    After: “That night, I went to sleep wondering if I would ever be tiny again.”

Grammar fixes

  1. 1. “Breath” vs. “breathe.” Breath (no e) is a noun — the air you take in. Breathe (with e) is the verb — the action. After “be able to,” you need the action word.

    Before: “I wouldn’t be able to breath.”
    After: “I wouldn’t be able to breathe.”

  2. 2. Every sentence starts with a capital letter. Your last sentence starts with a small t.

    Before: “then, I went to sleep”
    After: “Then I went to sleep” (or “That night, I went to sleep”)

  3. 3. One ant has two antennae. For insects, the plural of antenna is usually antennae (say “an-TEN-ee”). Antennas is used for TV and radio antennas.

    Before: “with its antennas”
    After: “with its antennae”

  4. 4. Leave out the extra “then.” In “because then I wouldn’t be able to,” the word because already explains the reason, so then isn’t needed.

    Before: “I was nervous because then I wouldn’t be able to breath.”
    After: “I was nervous because I wouldn’t be able to breathe.”
