Your answer

To escape something

Meaning: to leave a dangerous place (or move people out of it) to get to safety

Example: The town had to evacuate before the hurricane arrived.

You wrote “To escape something.” You are close: when people evacuate, they do get away. But evacuate means leaving a place because it is dangerous, usually in an organized way, often because someone in charge tells everyone to go (a fire drill, a flood, a storm). You can escape a bad dream or a boring chore, but you can’t evacuate them. Try: “to leave a dangerous place to stay safe.”

Words to practice

  • evacuate (verb) — to leave a dangerous place to get to safety

Memory tip: Evacuate has vac in it, like vacant (empty) and vacuum. When a building is evacuated, everyone leaves and it becomes empty — because it isn’t safe.
