The topic

Topic

A rainy day turns into a big adventure.

Type of writing

Narrative — Tell a story with a beginning, middle, and end.

How to write it

  1. Beginning: tell who is in the story, where they are, and why the rainy day seems boring at first.
  2. Middle: tell what happens that turns the day into an adventure. Use First, Then, and Suddenly to show the order.
  3. End: tell how the adventure finishes and how you felt about the rainy day after all.

Sentence starter

“When I looked out the window and saw the pouring rain, I thought the whole day was ruined.” (You can use this or write your own first sentence.)

Try to use these words

suddenly  ·  soaked  ·  splashing

What you did great

  • A twist in the middle. First the board games get boring, and then “I realized again that we could play outside in the rain.” That is a real turning point — the boring day flips into an adventure, which is exactly what the topic asked for.
  • An ending that answers the beginning. You start with “I thought the whole day was ruined” and finish with “the rain was actually very fun.” The word actually shows your feelings changed — that is how good stories end.

Let’s make it even better

Here is everything that will make your story stronger, starting with the most important. (Grammar is in its own section below.)

  1. 1. Give the adventure more than one sentence. The rain adventure is the most exciting part, but it gets only one sentence, while the board games get three. Readers want to see the fun: running into the yard, jumping in puddles, getting soaked. Spend your words on the best part.

    Before: “So we got our raincoats and we started splashing and we got soaked when we were done.”
    After: “We grabbed our raincoats and ran into the yard, splashing through every puddle until we were completely soaked.”

  2. 2. Save suddenly for the big surprise. Suddenly is a strong word, so use it at the moment everything changes — when you get the idea to go outside. Remembering your board games is not the big surprise. The plan also asked for First and Then to show order, so use those for the board games.

    Before: “But suddenly I remembered that I had a lot of board games.” / “But then I realized again that we could play outside in the rain.”
    After: “First, we pulled out my stack of board games and played round after round.” / “Suddenly, I had an idea: why not play outside in the rain?”

  3. 3. Don’t start so many sentences with But and So. Five of your nine sentences start with But or So. That makes the story sound like talking out loud. Use time words instead (First, Then, Suddenly, When), or just start with who did what.

    Before: “But suddenly…” “So I told…” “But it got…” “But then…” “So we got…”
    After: “First, we…” “Then even the games…” “Suddenly, I…” “We grabbed…”

  4. 4. A story narrator doesn’t need to say his name. In a story told with I, the reader already knows you are the one talking. Use that space to tell who is with you and why the day is boring. I joined sentences 2 and 3 into one smooth sentence.

    Before: “My name is Grant, and Me, Caleb, and Eli were all at my house for a play date. We thought the day was gonna be very boring because it was raining and we had to stay inside.”
    After: “My friends Caleb and Eli had come over for a play date, and now we were stuck inside.”

  5. 5. Say exactly what was boring. In “it got boring, so we thought it was over,” the reader has to guess what it means each time — the games? the play date? Name it and show it with an action.

    Before: “But it got boring, so we thought it was over.”
    After: “Then even the games got boring, and we flopped on the couch with nothing to do.”

  6. 6. Make the ending feel like an ending. Your last sentence has a great idea, but it puts two different things together with and (friends going home + the rain being fun). Tell how you felt, and connect it to the topic: the rainy day turned into an adventure.

    Before: “Then my friends went home and the rain was actually very fun.”
    After: “When my friends finally went home, I smiled, because the rainy day had turned into the best adventure of all.”

Grammar fixes

  1. 1. Use I (not Me) as the subject, and put yourself last. Test it by taking out the other names: you would say “I was at my house,” not “Me was.” Being polite, we name ourselves last. Also, me should not have a capital letter in the middle of a sentence.

    Before: “Me, Caleb, and Eli were all at my house”
    After: “Caleb, Eli, and I were all at my house”

  2. 2. Write going to, not gonna. Gonna is how we say it when we talk fast, but in writing we spell out the real words.

    Before: “the day was gonna be very boring”
    After: “the day was going to be very boring”

  3. 3. Too many ands make a run-on sentence. “We got our raincoats and we started splashing and we got soaked” strings three sentences together. Use a comma and an -ing word, or split it up.

    Before: “So we got our raincoats and we started splashing and we got soaked when we were done.”
    After: “We grabbed our raincoats and ran into the yard, splashing through every puddle until we were completely soaked.”

  4. 4. Put a comma before and when it joins two complete sentences. “I told my friends” and “we started to play” could each stand alone, so a comma goes before and.

    Before: “So I told my friends and we started to play some board games.” / “Then my friends went home and the rain was actually very fun.”
    After: “I told my friends, and we started to play some board games.” / “Then my friends went home, and the rain had actually been very fun.”

  5. 5. Make the time make sense. “We got soaked when we were done” sounds like you got wet after you stopped playing. You got soaked while you played, so say until or by the time.

    Before: “we got soaked when we were done”
    After: “until we were completely soaked” (or: “by the time we were done, we were soaked”)

Your spelling is perfect, and your first sentence uses a comma after the opening part (“…saw the pouring rain, I thought…”) exactly right.
