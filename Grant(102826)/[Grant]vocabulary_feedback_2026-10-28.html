Your answer

A place where something goes

Meaning: a group of things that are alike in some way

Example: At the library, books about animals and space are in the science category.

You wrote “A place where something goes.” You are close — things do get sorted into a category. But a category is not a place; it is a group or kind of things that share something. Apples, bananas, and grapes all belong to the category “fruit.” Try: “a group of things that are alike.”

To persuade someone to do something

Meaning: to make someone believe something or agree to do something

Example: Maya convinced her dad that a puppy would be good for the family.

Yes! Persuade is a perfect match. A convinced person also believes it: I am convinced it will rain.

Your answer

To draw something that’s not perfectly real

Meaning: to draw pictures for a book or story; also, to explain something by giving an example

Example: The teacher illustrated her point by showing us a real fossil.

You wrote “To draw something that’s not perfectly real.” Drawing is right — an illustrator draws the pictures in a book. But the pictures do not have to be unreal; they can show real things, too. And there is a second meaning: to explain by showing an example. Try: “to make pictures for a book, or to explain with an example.”

Words to practice

  • category — a group of things that are alike
  • illustrate — to make pictures for a book, or to explain with an example

Memory tip: Think of a picture book: the illustrator draws the pictures, and every book on the shelf belongs to a category like mystery, sports, or science.
