The topic

Topic

Which season is the best?

Type of writing

Opinion — Tell what you think and give reasons.

How to write it

  1. Say which season you think is the best.
  2. Give your first reason and an example (something you do, see, or feel in that season).
  3. Give your second reason and another example.
  4. Finish with a closing sentence that says your opinion again in a new way.

Sentence starter

“In my opinion, the best season of the year is (your season) because it is full of fun things to do.” (You can use this or write your own first sentence.)

Try to use these words

because  ·  crisp  ·  cozy

Task: Write one paragraph (60–120 words). Use the plan above and try to use the three words.

Your writing

In my opinion, the best season of the year is winter because it is full of fun things to do. What you did great

  • You gave two real reasons. Reason 1 is all the fun outside (“jump in the snow,” “sled,” “make snow angels”), and reason 2 starts with “Another reason,” which tells the reader a new reason is coming.
  • Your details use your senses. “Feel the snow on your body” and “enjoy the cozy blanket over your body” help the reader feel cold and then warm — that’s exactly what good opinion writers do.

Let’s make it even better

Here is everything that will make your paragraph stronger, starting with the most important. (Grammar mistakes are in their own section below.)

  1. 1. Give your first reason its own clear start. Your plan said: opinion, then reason 1, then reason 2. Your second sentence starts with “I think winter is the best season because” — almost the same words as your first sentence. Use a signal phrase so the reader knows reason 1 is starting.

    Before: “I think winter is the best season because you can jump in the snow…”
    After: “One reason is that you can jump in the snow…”

  2. 2. Use “crisp” for the right thing. Great try using the bonus word! But crisp means cool and fresh (like air) or crunchy (like an apple). Hot chocolate is warm and smooth, so it can’t be crisp. Also, “hot, crisp hot chocolate” says hot two times. Move crisp to the cold air outside.

    Before: “you can have hot, crisp hot chocolate”
    After: “you can sip hot chocolate … after the crisp winter air”

  3. 3. Name the fun things instead of “much more things you can do.” “Much more things you can do” is fuzzy. The reader can’t picture it. Either name one more activity or use a short, clear phrase.

    Before: “make a snow man, and much more things you can do”
    After: “make a snowman, and do many more fun activities”

  4. 4. Don’t say “snow” so many times. The word snow shows up seven times. In one sentence it is there twice: “feel the snow on your body as you make snow angels in the snow.” Swap some for other words like flakes, or just leave them out.

    Before: “feel the snow on your body as you make snow angels in the snow”
    After: “feel the cold flakes on your face as you make snow angels”

  5. 5. Say “inside” only once. Your fourth sentence says “when you go inside” and then “when you get inside” again at the end. One time is enough.

    Before: “and enjoy the cozy blanket over your body when you get inside”
    After: “and enjoy a cozy blanket over your body”

  6. 6. Make your closing sentence say it in a new way. The plan asked you to end by saying your opinion again in a new way. Your last sentence starts with the same words as your second sentence. Try to sum up both reasons (playing in the cold and getting cozy) in one fresh sentence.

    Before: “I think winter is the best season because you can get cozy after you were in the cold snow.”
    After: “Winter is the best season because you get to play in the cold and then warm up somewhere cozy.”

Grammar fixes

  1. 1. “Snowman” is one word. Some words are two small words stuck together. Snowman is one of them.

    Before: “make a snow man”
    After: “make a snowman”

  2. 2. Use “many” with things you can count. You can count things (one thing, two things), so use many, not much. Much is for things you can’t count, like much snow.

    Before: “much more things”
    After: “many more things”

  3. 3. Keep a list simple and parallel. When you list actions, say you can one time and then separate the actions with commas. Don’t restart with “and you can.”

    Before: “you can jump in the snow and you can sled, make a snow man, and much more things you can do”
    After: “you can jump in the snow, sled, make a snowman, and do many more fun activities”

  4. 4. “The reason is because” says the same thing twice. Reason and because both mean “why.” Write the reason is that instead, and drop why.

    Before: “Another reason why is because when you go inside”
    After: “Another reason is that when you go inside”

  5. 5. Put a comma after a “when” part at the start. When a sentence part that starts with when comes first, put a comma after it.

    Before: “when you go inside you can have”
    After: “when you go inside, you can have”

  6. 6. Keep the times matching. “You can get cozy” is about now, so “after you were” (past) doesn’t match. Use after you have been, or just say after playing.

    Before: “after you were in the cold snow”
    After: “after you have been out in the cold snow”

Every sentence starts with a capital letter and ends with a period, and all your other words are spelled right. Nice!

Did you use the three words?

  • because — Yes! “…winter because it is full of fun things to do.”
  • crisp — Yes, you used it! But it described the hot chocolate. Next time, use crisp for cold air or crunchy food (see tip 2 above).
  • cozy — Yes! “enjoy the cozy blanket” and “get cozy.” Great job using all three!
