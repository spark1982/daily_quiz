The topic

Topic

Should recess be longer?

Type of writing

Opinion — Tell what you think and give reasons.

How to write it

  1. Start by telling your opinion: should recess be longer, or is it long enough?
  2. Give your first reason and an example from your own school day.
  3. Give your second reason and another example.
  4. End with a closing sentence that says your opinion again in a new way.

Sentence starter

“I think recess should be longer because kids need more time to move and play.” (You can use this or write your own first sentence.)

Try to use these words

because  ·  energetic  ·  focused

Task: Write one paragraph (60–120 words). Use the plan above and try to use the three words.

What you did great

  • Your opinion comes first. “I think recess should be longer because kids need more time to move and play.” The reader knows your opinion from the very first sentence — that is exactly how an opinion paragraph should start.
  • Your reasons are about real kids. Having to stop “in the middle of a game” and still being “very energetic” when recess ends are things every kid understands. Reasons like these are easy for a reader to agree with.

Let’s make it even better

Here is everything that will make your paragraph stronger, starting with the most important. (Grammar is in its own section below.)

  1. 1. Add a real example for your first reason. The plan asked for “an example from your own school day.” Your first reason says kids might be in the middle of a game, but it does not show a moment. One short example makes a reason much stronger. I added an example sentence — next time, use a true moment from your recess.

    Before: (no example)
    After: “For example, the bell sometimes rings right before someone wins a game of tag, and everyone has to stop.”

  2. 2. Don’t repeat your opinion inside every reason. You wrote “I think recess should be longer” three times. Say your opinion once at the start and once (in new words) at the end. In the middle, just give the reason and what would get better.

    Before: “…so I think recess should be longer so they can finish their game…”
    After: “With a longer recess, kids could finish their games and come back to class more focused.”

  3. 3. Start your second reason the same way as your first. You started reason 1 with “My first reason is that…” — great! Reason 2 should match: “My second reason is that…”. Matching starts help the reader see your plan.

    Before: “The second reason, they might be very energetic, so they might still be silly on the way in.”
    After: “My second reason is that many kids are still very energetic when recess is over, so they act silly on the way back inside.”

  4. 4. Say who they are. In “they might be very energetic,” the reader has to guess who they means. When you start a new reason, name the people again (many kids). Also stick with one word: you used kids and then switched to people.

    Before: “they might be very energetic” / “so people can get their energy out”
    After: “many kids are still very energetic” / “More time outside would let them get their energy out”

  5. 5. Add a real closing sentence. Your last sentence finishes reason 2, so the paragraph stops without an ending. The plan asked for a closing sentence that says your opinion again in a new way. I added one.

    Before: (no closing sentence)
    After: “A few extra minutes of recess would help kids play hard first and then learn hard.”

Grammar fixes

  1. 1. Break up a run-on sentence. Your second sentence joins four ideas with but, so, so, and and. When a sentence keeps going with so… so… and…, stop and make two sentences.

    Before: “My first reason is that kids might be in the middle of a game but they have to stop so I think recess should be longer so they can finish their game and they can be more focused.”
    After: “My first reason is that kids are often in the middle of a game when recess ends. With a longer recess, kids could finish their games and come back to class more focused.”

  2. 2. Put a comma before but when it joins two sentences. “Kids might be in the middle of a game” and “they have to stop” could each be a sentence. When but, and, or so joins two sentences, a comma goes in front of it.

    Before: “kids might be in the middle of a game but they have to stop”
    After: “kids might be in the middle of a game, but they have to stop”

  3. 3. “The second reason, they…” is not a complete start. “The second reason” has no verb to go with it, so the sentence starts with a piece that is not finished. Add is that to make it complete.

    Before: “The second reason, they might be very energetic…”
    After: “My second reason is that many kids are still very energetic…”

  • energetic — Yes! “…they might be very energetic…”
  • focused — Yes! “…they can be more focused.” All three words — great job!
