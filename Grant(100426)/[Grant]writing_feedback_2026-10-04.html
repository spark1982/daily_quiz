The topic

Topic

You become invisible for one day. What happens?

Type of writing

Narrative — Tell a story with a beginning, middle, and end.

How to write it

  1. Beginning: Tell who you are, where you are, and how you find out you are invisible.
  2. Middle: Tell what you do while you are invisible. Pick one or two exciting events and tell them in order.
  3. Add details: What do you see, hear, or feel? How do other people react?
  4. End: Tell how the day ends and how you feel about it.

Sentence starter

“When I looked in the mirror on Saturday morning, nobody looked back at me.” (You can use this or write your own first sentence.)

Try to use these words

suddenly  ·  whispered  ·  mysterious

Task: Write one paragraph (60–120 words). Use the plan above and try to use the three words.

What you did great

  • You started strong. You used the sentence starter and then told the reader how you felt: “I was confused because usually I show in the mirror.” That makes the reader want to keep going.
  • Your events are in order. You looked in the mirror, went for a walk, played a video game, and went to sleep. The reader can always follow along, and your word count (105) is right in the 60–120 range.

Let’s make it even better

Here is everything that will make your story stronger, starting with the most important. (Grammar mistakes are in their own section below.)

  1. 1. Add one or two exciting events. The plan asked you to pick one or two exciting things that happen while you are invisible. Taking a walk and playing a video game are things you could do on any day. Being invisible is a superpower, so show it! What could you do that you could never do before?

    Before: “So after my walk, I played a video game.”
    After: “At the park, I sneaked up behind my friend Max and whispered his name. He jumped and looked all around, but he could not see anyone!”

  2. 2. Show what you see, hear, and feel. Details help the reader feel like they are there with you. Instead of only saying what happened, tell what people did and how you felt.

    Before: “I tried greeting people, but they didn’t even look back at me.”
    After: “I waved and said hello to a lady walking her dog, but she walked right past me. Her dog sniffed the air and barked at nothing. I felt lonely and a little bit excited at the same time.”

  3. 3. Make sure your sentences do not argue with each other. “I was normal like other people, but I was invisible” is confusing, because an invisible person is not like other people. Say what you really mean.

    Before: “I was normal like other people, but i was invisible.”
    After: “I did normal things, but nobody could see me do them.”

  4. 4. Say each idea only once. Your first sentence already tells the reader you were looking in the mirror. The fourth sentence says it again. Use that space for something new instead.

    Before: “I was at my house, looking in the mirror because i wanted to see how my hair looked.”
    After: “I was at my house, trying to fix my hair.”

  5. 5. Use clear, everyday phrases. “To get my brain out of it” is not a phrase readers know. Use a phrase that clearly says what you mean.

    Before: “I went out for a walk to get my brain out of it.”
    After: “I went out for a walk to clear my head.”

  6. 6. Give your story a real ending. The plan asked how the day ends and how you feel about it. Going to sleep is a fine ending, but tell the reader your feeling too.

    Before: “I finally went to sleep and ended the day.”
    After: “That night, I went to sleep and wondered if I would be invisible again tomorrow. Being invisible was mysterious, but I was happy to be seen again.”

Grammar fixes

  1. 1. Always write “I” as a capital letter. When you talk about yourself, the word I is always a capital letter, even in the middle of a sentence. You wrote a small “i” two times.

    Before: “because i wanted to see how my hair looked”
    After: “because I wanted to see how my hair looked”

  2. 2. Capital “I” again. Same rule here.

    Before: “but i was invisible”
    After: “but I was invisible”

  3. 3. Add the missing little word “up.” We say a person shows up in a mirror. Also, words like usually go right before the action word.

    Before: “usually I show in the mirror”
    After: “I usually show up in the mirror”

Did you use the three words?

  • suddenly — Not yet. Try: “Suddenly, a dog barked at me, even though nobody could see me.”
  • whispered — Not yet. Try: “I whispered my friend’s name, and he jumped.”
  • mysterious — Not yet. Try: “It was a mysterious day that I will never forget.”
