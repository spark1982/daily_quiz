The flaw in the argument

Model answer

Judge, my opponent’s whole case rests on a post hoc fallacy. They list problems that came after the Millbrook data center opened — higher bills, a low creek, closed diners, even a losing football season — and assume the data center caused all of them. Timing is not proof. Creeks drop in dry summers, energy prices rise for many reasons, and restaurants close all the time. My opponent never compares Millbrook to similar towns without a data center, so we have no evidence that the building caused any of these changes.

Even where a real cost exists, a nationwide moratorium is the wrong answer. If a data center strains the power grid or a creek, the fix is local: require the company to pay for new power lines, use recycled water, or cool its servers with outside air. Many new centers already do this. Stopping all construction would block the computers that hospitals use to read X-rays faster and that weather services use to predict storms.

So the Affirmative proves only that some things happened after a building opened, not that the building caused them, and their solution would throw away real benefits instead of fixing real problems. Please vote Negative.
