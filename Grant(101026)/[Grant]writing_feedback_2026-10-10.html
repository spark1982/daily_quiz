The topic

Topic

Describe your favorite place in your town and what people do there.

Type of writing

Informative — Teach the reader about something, step by step.

How to write it

  1. Name your favorite place and tell where it is in your town.
  2. Explain what people do there, in order: First, Next, Then, Finally.
  3. Add details: What do you see, hear, or smell there?
  4. End with a sentence that tells why this place is your favorite.

Sentence starter

“My favorite place in my town is ___, which is near ___.” (You can use this or write your own first sentence.)

Try to use these words

next  ·  crowded  ·  peaceful

Task: Write one paragraph (60–120 words). Use the plan above and try to use the three words.

Your writing

My favorite place in town is Walmart which is in Olathe. First, it is there so people can buy stuff that they need. Next, there is a lot of different stuff which is cool to look at. What you did great

  • You used the order words just like the plan. “First,” “Next,” “Then,” and “Finally” each start a sentence, so the reader can follow a whole trip to the store, all the way to “you check out and drive home and enjoy what you bought.”
  • Your ending tells why it is your favorite. “This place is my favorite because it is calm” answers the last step of the plan, and “like its a never-ending place” is a fun way to show how big the store feels.

Let’s make it even better

Here is everything that will make your paragraph stronger, starting with the most important. (Grammar mistakes are in their own section below.)

  1. 1. Make every step something people do. Your plan asked you to explain what people do there, in order. Your “First” sentence tells why the store is there, not what people do first. Start with the very first thing a shopper does when they walk in.

    Before: “First, it is there so people can buy stuff that they need.”
    After: “First, people grab a cart at the front door and check their shopping list.”

  2. 2. Trade “stuff” for exact words. You used “stuff” two times. “Stuff” could mean anything, so the reader can’t picture it. Name real things a person would see at Walmart.

    Before: “Next, there is a lot of different stuff which is cool to look at.”
    After: “Next, they walk down long aisles full of toys, video games, and school supplies.”

  3. 3. Add what you see, hear, or smell. The plan asked for details from your senses, and that part is missing. One sense detail makes the reader feel like they are standing in the store with you.

    Before: “Then, there are food, clothes, and more.”
    After: “Then, they pick out food and clothes while the smell of fresh bread drifts from the bakery and the checkout scanners beep.”

  4. 4. Make your reason match your feeling. “Peaceful because there is just so much to look at” is confusing, because lots of things to look at does not make a place quiet. Tell the real reason it feels peaceful.

    Before: “It isn’t that crowded, and also peaceful because there is just so much to look at.”
    After: “It isn’t that crowded, so it feels peaceful as I walk down the quiet aisles.”

  5. 5. Stick with one kind of person. Most sentences talk about “people,” but the “Finally” sentence suddenly switches to “you.” Keep the same word all the way through so your steps sound connected.

    Before: “Finally, you check out and drive home and enjoy what you bought.”
    After: “Finally, they check out, drive home, and enjoy what they bought.”

  6. 6. Don’t repeat the same phrase. “To look at” appears three times. Using a new verb at the end makes your last sentence stronger.

    Before: “has many things to look at like its a never-ending place.”
    After: “has so many things to explore that it feels like a never-ending place.”

Grammar fixes

  1. 1. Use a comma before “which” when it adds extra information. “Which is in Olathe” is an extra fact about Walmart, so put a comma before which.

    Before: “My favorite place in town is Walmart which is in Olathe.”
    After: “My favorite place in town is Walmart, which is in Olathe.”

  2. 2. “There is” or “there are” matches the first thing in the list. The first item in your list is food, which is one thing, so use there is.

    Before: “Then, there are food, clothes, and more.”
    After: “Then, there is food, clothing, and more.”

  3. 3. “It’s” means “it is.” When you mean it is, you need an apostrophe: it’s. Without it, its means “belonging to it.”

    Before: “like its a never-ending place”
    After: “like it’s a never-ending place”

  4. 4. Use commas in a list of actions. When you list three or more actions, put commas between them instead of repeating and.

    Before: “you check out and drive home and enjoy what you bought”
    After: “you check out, drive home, and enjoy what you bought”

  5. 5. No comma before “and” when the subject is shared. “It is calm” and “has many things” share the same subject (it), so you don’t need a comma before and.

    Before: “because it is calm, and has many things”
    After: “because it is calm and has many things”

Your spelling is correct all the way through, and every sentence starts with a capital letter and ends with a period. Great work!
