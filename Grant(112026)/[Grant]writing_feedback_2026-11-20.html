The topic

Topic

Should every student learn to play a musical instrument?

Type of writing

Opinion — Tell what you think and give reasons.

How to write it

  1. Start by telling your opinion: yes or no?
  2. Give two reasons. Use First and Also to start them.
  3. Give an example for each reason, like something that happened to you or a friend.
  4. End with a closing sentence that says your opinion again in new words.

Try to use these words

because  ·  patient  ·  confident

Task: Write one paragraph (60–120 words). Use the plan above and try to use the three words.

Your writing

I think every student shouldn’t play a musical instrument. First, people might not enjoy the instrument in the first place. Also, students might be too busy to play instruments because the student might be very stressed about having to focus on their things and the instrument. For example, none of my friends have been stressed, but someone can get very stressed and not be able to learn fast because they can’t practice. What you did great

  • You followed the plan for reasons. “First, people might not enjoy the instrument” and “Also, students might be too busy” are two different reasons, and the words First and Also help the reader see where each one starts.
  • You thought about how other kids feel. Ideas like being “very stressed” and not feeling “confident in their playing” show that you were thinking about real students, not just yourself. That makes an opinion more convincing.

Let’s make it even better

Here is everything that will make your paragraph stronger, starting with the most important. (Grammar is in its own section below.)

  1. 1. Make your opinion sentence say exactly what you mean. The topic asks whether every student should learn an instrument. Your answer is “no, not every student should have to.” But “every student shouldn’t play” sounds like no student should ever play an instrument — which is not what the rest of your paragraph says. Use the words from the question so your opinion is crystal clear.

    Before: “I think every student shouldn’t play a musical instrument.”
    After: “I don’t think every student should have to learn a musical instrument.”

  2. 2. Pick an example that helps your reason, not one that argues against it. Your example says “none of my friends have been stressed” — but your reason is that students get stressed! An example should prove your reason. If you don’t have a real story, describe what would happen to a busy student.

    Before: “For example, none of my friends have been stressed, but someone can get very stressed and not be able to learn fast because they can’t practice.”
    After: “For example, a student who has no time to practice can’t improve, and that student might stop feeling confident.”

    Notice that the new example also uses your sentence about not feeling confident, so two of your ideas now work together.

  3. 3. Use exact words instead of “their things.” “Their things” doesn’t let the reader picture anything. Name the things that keep students busy. Also, your long sentence held two ideas (busy, and stressed); splitting it into two sentences makes each idea clear.

    Before: “Also, students might be too busy to play instruments because the student might be very stressed about having to focus on their things and the instrument.”
    After: “Also, many students are already busy with homework, sports, and other activities. Adding daily practice could make them feel very stressed.”

  4. 4. Make every sentence say one clear idea. “Some people can also be not patient and try to learn so they can do other things” is hard to follow — the reader can’t tell what “try to learn so they can do other things” means. Decide what you want to say about patience, and say it plainly.

    Before: “Some people can also be not patient and try to learn so they can do other things.”
    After: “Learning an instrument takes patient practice every day, and not every student has the time for it.”

  5. 5. In your closing, say your opinion again in new words. Your last sentence is a good closing! It repeats your first reason (“they might not want to”), though. A strong closing restates the opinion in a fresh way — like calling it “a choice, not a rule.”

    Before: “Students shouldn’t have to play an instrument because they might not want to anyway.”
    After: “That is why I believe an instrument should be a choice, not a rule, because students learn best when they want to.”

Grammar fixes

  1. 1. Put not in the right place. “Every student shouldn’t” means all students should not. “Not every student should” means some should and some don’t have to. Where you put not changes the meaning!

    Before: “every student shouldn’t play”
    After: “not every student should have to learn”

  2. 2. Keep talking about the same group. In one sentence you wrote students (many), then the student (one), then their (many). Pick one and stay with it. Plural is easiest: students … they … their.

    Before: “students might be too busy … because the student might be very stressed … their things”
    After: “students are already busy … could make them feel very stressed”

  3. 3. Say “impatient” or “not be patient,” not “be not patient.” In English, not goes right after a helping verb like can: “can not be,” or you can use the one-word opposite, impatient.

    Before: “Some people can also be not patient”
    After: “Some people can also be impatient”

  4. 4. Don’t say the same word twice in one sentence when you don’t need to. “people might not enjoy the instrument in the first place” already starts with First, so “in the first place” repeats first. Leave it out.

    Before: “First, people might not enjoy the instrument in the first place.”
    After: “First, some students might not enjoy playing an instrument at all.”

Did you use the three words?

  • because — Yes! You used it three times, for example “because they don’t have time to practice.”
  • patient — Yes! “be not patient” — next time try “impatient” or “patient practice” (see Grammar fix #3).
  • confident — Yes! “They might not be confident in their playing”

I don’t think every student should have to learn a musical instrument. First, some students might not enjoy playing an instrument at all.
