Your answer

Judge, this is the ad hominem fallacy. The Affirmative's only real argument attacks the zoo director, not the statement they made. The fact that the director earns a large salary mainly funded by concessions and tickets does not make it reasonable to say that the director's statements must be thrown out. You cannot simply assume that if a director's income mainly comes from one source, they are completely irrelevant for everything else! They still supervise the zoo, and there is a real, very likely chance that they truly care about the animals! The Affirmative ignores that zoos are essential for many endangered species. Zoos keep the polar bears in enclosures because the enclosures are safer! They can be monitored and cared for in these enclosures! EVEN IF we look from a very pessimistic perspective and assume zoos only operate in self-interest, they STILL must ensure that they are at LEAST keeping their animals alive, since the Affirmative says "700,00 people visit the Riverside City Zoo" annually! They need this for their income, and this is the WORST case scenario. More often than not, animals held in captivity are safer in enclosures than in the wild. Unless saving animals is immoral, the Affirmative's argument is not the case. The judge should vote Negative.

The flaw in the argument

Ad Hominem — attacking the person making a claim instead of the claim itself. The opponent pointed to the director’s salary, the zoo paying its scientists, and a car used for a vacation, then concluded that “their claims about conservation should be thrown out.” None of those facts shows that zoo conservation programs fail.

Model answer

Judge, my opponent never proves that zoos fail to save species. Instead, they attack the zoo director’s salary and her use of a zoo car. This is the ad hominem fallacy: it targets the person instead of the claim. Even if the director earns a large salary, that tells us nothing about whether zoo breeding programs work. A claim stands or falls on its evidence, and my opponent offers none against the scientists’ findings.

Now look at the evidence for my side. Accredited zoos run Species Survival Plans that manage breeding for hundreds of endangered animals. The California condor shows why this matters: in 1987, only 27 condors were left, and all of them were brought into zoo breeding programs. Today more than 500 condors exist, and most of them fly free in the wild. Without zoos, that species would likely be gone.

My opponent is right that animal welfare matters, and a pacing polar bear deserves a better enclosure. But that is a reason to improve zoos, not to call them immoral. When the wild itself is shrinking because of a warming Arctic, zoos offer a safety net for species that would otherwise disappear. Because the Affirmative attacks people instead of evidence, and because zoos are rescuing species from extinction, please vote Negative.
