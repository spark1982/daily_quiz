The topic

Topic

Is it better to play sports or video games after school?

Type of writing

Opinion — Tell what you think and give reasons.

How to write it

  1. Start by telling your opinion: sports or video games?
  2. Give two reasons. Add an example for each reason (something that really happened to you or a friend).
  3. End with a closing sentence that says your opinion again in new words.

Try to use these words

In addition  ·  energetic  ·  challenging

What you did great

  • Your opinion is thoughtful. “People should play sports before playing video games” shows you thought about both choices and found a smart order for them. That is more interesting than just picking one.
  • You used real examples. “One time I was playing soccer with my dad, and it was really fun beating him” is exactly what the plan asked for — something that really happened to you.

Let’s make it even better

Here is everything that will make your paragraph stronger, starting with the most important. (Grammar is in its own section below.)

  1. 1. Make your opinion sentence clear and smooth. Your first sentence should tell the reader your whole opinion in one clear idea. “But then they can play video games after” sounds like a second opinion. Say the order right away.

    Before: “I think people should play sports after school, but then they can play video games after.”
    After: “I think kids should play sports after school first and save video games for later.”

  2. 2. Split your first reason into two clear ideas. Your first reason has two ideas squeezed together, and the reader can’t tell what “it can get boring” means. Give each idea its own sentence: (1) sports burn off energy so you feel calm, and (2) video games all afternoon get boring.

    Before: “First, people should play sports because it can get your energetic self to calm and then you can play some video games after because it can get boring.”
    After: “First, sports help energetic kids burn off extra energy, so they feel calm when they finally sit down. Then video games feel more fun, because playing them all afternoon can get boring.”

  3. 3. Start your example with “For example.” Your story about playing sports after school is an example of your first reason. Telling the reader “For example” shows how it connects. Also say what happened in a clearer way than “got me to get my energy out.”

    Before: “After school, I once played some sports, and it really got me to get my energy out, and I of course played some video games after.”
    After: “For example, one day after school I played sports until my energy was gone, and then, of course, I enjoyed a few video games.”

  4. 4. Use the helper words “In addition” and “challenging.” “Second” works, but the plan gave you In addition, which is a great way to add a reason. The soccer game with your dad is the perfect place for challenging — beating your dad is hard, and that is why it is fun.

    Before: “Second, it can also sometimes be more fun even than playing video games, because one time I was playing soccer with my dad, and it was really fun beating him.”
    After: “In addition, sports can be even more fun than video games. One time I played soccer with my dad, and it was challenging, but beating him was really fun.”

  5. 5. Don’t repeat the same word too many times. You used after six times and get or got five times. Try other words: later, then, first, burn off, enjoy. Your writing will sound more grown-up.

    Before: “then they can play video games after”
    After: “save video games for later”

  6. 6. Make your closing sentence say your opinion in new words. Your last sentence almost repeats your first one. A strong closing gives the reader one last reason to agree, in new words.

    Before: “People should play sports before playing video games because it can help get energy out of them.”
    After: “That is why kids should play sports before they play video games: a good game outside gets the energy out and makes the rest of the day better.”

Grammar fixes

Every grammar, spelling, capital-letter, and punctuation fix, one by one.

  1. 1. Don’t switch from “people” to “you.” You started talking about people, then changed to your and you in the same sentence. Pick one and keep it. (Kids … they works well.)

    Before: “people should play sports because it can get your energetic self to calm and then you can play”
    After: “sports help energetic kids burn off extra energy, so they feel calm”

  2. 2. Break up a sentence that runs on. When one sentence has because … and then … because, it gets too long to follow. End the first idea with a period and start a new sentence.

    Before: “…to calm and then you can play some video games after because it can get boring.”
    After: “…when they finally sit down. Then video games feel more fun, because playing them all afternoon can get boring.”

  3. 3. Put commas around “of course.” Of course is an extra phrase in the middle of a sentence, so it goes between two commas, and it sounds best right before the subject.

    Before: “and I of course played some video games after.”
    After: “and then, of course, I enjoyed a few video games.”

  4. 4. Fix the word order: “even more fun than.” The word even goes before more, not between fun and than.

    Before: “it can also sometimes be more fun even than playing video games”
    After: “sports can be even more fun than video games”

  5. 5. Make it clear what “it” means. In “it can help get energy out of them,” the reader has to guess what it is. Name it: a good game outside or playing sports.

    Before: “because it can help get energy out of them.”
    After: “a good game outside gets the energy out”

Did you use the three words?

  • In addition — Not this time. You wrote “Second,” which is fine, but “In addition, sports can be even more fun” would add your reason in a stronger way.
  • energetic — Yes! “your energetic self” — nice try. It sounds even better describing people: “sports help energetic kids burn off extra energy.”
  • challenging — Not this time. Your soccer game with your dad was a perfect place for it: “it was challenging, but beating him was really fun.”
