Passage 1

1For centuries, geographic distance determined the boundaries of human community.
2Families separated by oceans could only exchange letters that took weeks to arrive.
3Today, digital technology has dissolved these barriers, enabling people to maintain
4meaningful relationships regardless of physical location. A grandmother in Seoul
5watches her grandchildren grow up in real time; a research team spread across
6three continents collaborates as fluidly as if they shared an office. Critics who
7argue that online interactions are inherently superficial misunderstand what connection
8fundamentally requires. It is not physical proximity, but shared attention, mutual
9vulnerability, and consistent engagement — qualities that digital platforms can facilitate
10just as readily as face-to-face encounters. The democratization of communication
11technology has, in fact, expanded the pool of potential meaningful relationships beyond
12anything previous generations could have imagined, enriching rather than diminishing
13human social life.

Passage 2

14The smartphone generation has inherited a paradox: we are more connected than
15any humans in history, yet surveys consistently report that loneliness is rising. This
16apparent contradiction dissolves when we examine the quality, rather than the quantity,
17of our interactions. Digital platforms engineer engagement through novelty and reward
18cycles, optimizing for time-on-screen rather than for depth of relationship. The
19conversations that build genuine trust — vulnerable, slow, and often uncomfortable —
20are precisely the type that digital communication tends to abbreviate or avoid entirely.
21We mistake the frictionless exchange of information for intimacy, and accumulate
22contacts while starving for authentic connection. A community requires more than a
23shared feed; it demands shared risk, shared silence, and the willingness to remain
24present through difficulty. Until technology is designed to cultivate depth rather than
25breadth, it will continue to substitute the appearance of connection for the reality.
