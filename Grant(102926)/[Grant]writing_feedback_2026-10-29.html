The topic

Topic

You travel 100 years into the future in a time machine.

Type of writing

Narrative — Tell a story with a beginning, middle, and end.

How to write it

  1. Beginning: tell who is in the story and how the time machine trip starts.
  2. Middle: tell what you see and do in the future, in order. Use time words like first, then, and after that.
  3. Add one detail that shows how you feel (what you say, do, or think).
  4. End: tell how the story ends and how you felt when it was over.

Sentence starter

“When I stepped out of the time machine, I could not believe my eyes.” (You can use this or write your own first sentence.)

Try to use these words

after that  ·  amazed  ·  gleaming

Task: Write one paragraph (60–120 words). Use the plan above and try to use the three words.

What you did great

  • Time words that keep the story moving. You used “First,” “Next,” “After that,” and “Then” in order, so the reader always knows what happens next — exactly what the plan asked.
  • A surprising, funny detail. “There were barely any humans and more robots” and finding Five Guys 100 years later make the future feel strange and familiar. And “I was amazed at how freshly cooked the food was” shows a real feeling.

Let’s make it even better

Here is everything that will make your story stronger, starting with the most important. (Grammar is in its own section below.)

  1. 1. Start with who and how the trip begins. Your plan said the beginning should tell who is in the story and how the time machine trip starts. Right now you step out of the machine first and then tell the reader your name and that you traveled — so the trip seems to happen twice. Put the introduction first, then step out and be amazed.

    Before: “When I stepped out of the machine, I could not believe my eyes. My name is Grant, and I just walked out of a Time Machine that made me travel 100 years into the future.”
    After: “My name is Grant, and one Saturday I climbed into a time machine that carried me 100 years into the future. When I stepped out of the machine, I could not believe my eyes.”

  2. 2. Show what “fancy” looks like. Fancy is a fuzzy word — the reader can’t picture it. Your next sentence already has the picture (tall, gleaming buildings), so join the two sentences and let the details do the work. Also, you are standing in one place, so say buildings, not “rich cities.”

    Before: “First, everything was so fancy. There were many tall buildings and rich cities which were gleaming that I was not used to.”
    After: “First, everything looked so fancy, with tall buildings gleaming in the sunlight.”

  3. 3. Make the robot sentence compare clearly. “Barely any humans and more robots” mixes two different ways of counting. Use but to show the surprise: few people, lots of robots.

    Before: “Next, I noticed that there were barely any humans and more robots.”
    After: “Next, I noticed that there were barely any humans but lots of robots.”

  4. 4. Name the restaurant the easy way. “I went to a restaurant, and the name was 5 Guys, and I recognized it” chains three ideas together with and. Saying “a restaurant called Five Guys” is shorter and smoother. And “from before traveling” is clearer as “from my own time.”

    Before: “After that, I went to a restaurant, and the name was 5 Guys, and I recognized it from before traveling.”
    After: “After that, I went to a restaurant called Five Guys, and I recognized it from my own time.”

  5. 5. End with how you felt. The plan asked you to tell how the story ends and how you felt when it was over. Your last sentence tells what you did, so add one more sentence about your feeling. (Remember, your paragraph can be a little longer than 120 words if it needs to be.)

    Before: “(no feeling at the end)”
    After: “On the ride home, I could not stop smiling because I had seen the future.”

Grammar fixes

  1. 1. Don’t capitalize ordinary nouns. Time machine is not a name, so it does not get capital letters (just like car or bike).

    Before: “a Time Machine”
    After: “a time machine”

  2. 2. Add the missing the. Before a singular thing like machine, English needs a little word such as the or my.

    Before: “went back to machine”
    After: “went back to the machine”

  3. 3. Untangle the which … that sentence. “Cities which were gleaming that I was not used to” has two describing parts stacked on top of each other, so it is hard to read. Use just one describing part, or a short with phrase.

    Before: “There were many tall buildings and rich cities which were gleaming that I was not used to.”
    After: “First, everything looked so fancy, with tall buildings gleaming in the sunlight.”

  4. 4. Write the restaurant’s name the way it is spelled. The restaurant spells its name with a word: Five Guys. When you write a name, copy it exactly.

    Before: “5 Guys”
    After: “Five Guys”

Every time word has its comma, and all your other words are spelled correctly — nice!

Did you use the three words?

  • after that — Yes! “After that, I went to a restaurant…”
  • amazed — Yes! “I was amazed at how freshly cooked the food was.”
  • gleaming — Yes! “…which were gleaming…” In the polished version it describes the buildings directly: “tall buildings gleaming in the sunlight.”
