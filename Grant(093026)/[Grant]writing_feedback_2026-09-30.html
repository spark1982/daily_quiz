What you did great

  • You started with your opinion. “I think a dog is better because…” tells the reader your answer in the very first sentence. That is exactly how opinion writing should begin.
  • You were careful and honest. You wrote that dogs “mostly listen to you if you train them.” The words mostly and if you train them show you thought about what is really true. Good writers do that!

Let’s make it even better

Here is everything that will make your ideas and your paragraph stronger, starting with the most important. (Grammar mistakes are in their own section below.)

  1. 1. Give an example for each reason. Your plan asked for a reason and an example. A reason says why. An example shows it, so the reader can picture it. Try starting with “For example,” right after a reason.

    Before: “They are playful, and mostly listen to you if you train them.”
    After: “Dogs are playful. For example, they love to chase a ball and play fetch with you.”

  2. 2. Make your reason match the word loyal. Loyal means a friend who sticks with you and is always on your side. Being playful and listening are good things, but they do not show that a dog is loyal. Give a reason that really shows loyalty.

    Before: “They are also loyal, because they act very playful, they listen to you, and they can be a best friend.”
    After: “They are also loyal, because they stay by your side and are always happy to see you when you come home.”

  3. 3. Don’t repeat the same words over and over. “Playful” shows up 3 times, “listen to you” 3 times, and “best friend” 2 times in a short paragraph. When a word repeats, the reader stops learning anything new. Say each idea once, then add a new detail.

    Before: “… because they act very playful, they listen to you, and they can be a best friend.”
    After: “… because they stay by your side and are always happy to see you.”

  4. 4. Pick a reason that makes dogs better than cats. The topic is “a dog or a cat.” Cats are furry too, so “furry” does not show why a dog wins. The strongest reasons are things a dog does that a cat usually doesn’t. You can even name the cat side once.

    Before: “I think a dog is better because they are furry, and can be a best friend.”
    After: “Cats are cute, but I think a dog makes a better pet because it wants to be your best friend.”

  5. 5. Use the words of the question in your first sentence. The question asks which makes a better pet. Using the same words shows right away that you are answering it.

    Before: “I think a dog is better”
    After: “I think a dog makes a better pet”

  6. 6. Say your opinion again in a new way at the end. Your plan said to end by saying your opinion again in a new way. Your last sentence repeats the same two reasons with the same words. Try wrapping it up with a fresh, short sentence.

    Before: “Thats why I think dogs are better because they listen to you and they are very playful.”
    After: “That’s why a dog is the perfect pet for me: a playful, loyal friend who is always on my side.”

Grammar fixes

These are the grammar and punctuation mistakes in your paragraph. Each one has a short rule to remember.

  1. 1. Match a dog with it, or dogs with they. “A dog” is one animal, so use it. If you want to use they, say dogs. Keep them matching.

    Before: “I think a dog is better because they are furry”
    After: “I think a dog is better because it is furry”

  2. 2. Put the apostrophe in That’s. That’s is short for that is. The apostrophe ( ’ ) takes the place of the missing letter i.

    Before: “Thats why I think dogs are better”
    After: “That’s why I think dogs are better”

  3. 3. No comma when and joins two actions of the same subject. In “they are furry and can be a best friend,” both parts belong to the same they, so you don’t need a comma before and. The same goes for “They are playful and mostly listen to you.”

    Before: “they are furry, and can be a best friend.”
    After: “they are furry and can be a best friend.”

Did you use the three words?

  • ✅ loyal — yes! Next time, give a reason that really shows loyalty (see improvement 2).
  • ✅ playful — yes! Once is enough, though (see improvement 3).
