The flaw in the argument

Genetic Fallacy — judging an idea or thing as good or bad only because of where it came from. The opponent argued that because the internet, GPS, and the microwave began in military projects, they “will always carry the purpose of war inside them.” But an invention’s origin does not determine how it can be used later.

Model answer

Judge, my opponent’s entire case rests on one idea: because the internet, GPS, and the microwave started in military projects, they “will always carry the purpose of war inside them.” This is the genetic fallacy, judging something only by where it came from. The origin of a technology does not decide how people use it today. My opponent’s own example proves it: the microwave came from radar research, yet millions of families use it every day simply to heat dinner.

Now look at GPS. It was built to guide troops, but according to GPS.gov it now keeps banking systems and power grids running and “saves lives” by speeding search and rescue and emergency services. When a lost hiker is found or an ambulance takes the fastest route, that is a military invention being used ethically.

Yes, some people spread lies online or misuse tracking. But misuse by some does not prove humanity cannot use these tools ethically; it shows why we write privacy laws and safety rules, which we continue to do. Because people use these technologies ethically every day, our ability has kept pace with our progress. Please vote Negative.
