12The enthusiasm for redesigning cities around pedestrians and green space, while
13understandable, often glosses over the complex economic trade-offs involved. Reducing
14road capacity or converting parking lots into parks can impose real costs on small
15businesses that depend on car-accessible customers. Moreover, high-profile urban
16redesign projects have a documented history of accelerating gentrification: as
17neighborhoods become more attractive, property values rise and lower-income
18residents are displaced. The communities that urban planners claim to serve are
19sometimes the first to be priced out of the very spaces they were promised. A more
20honest accounting would acknowledge that beautification and equity do not always
21advance together, and that good intentions alone are insufficient to ensure just outcomes.
