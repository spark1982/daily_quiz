Event Situation

You are to assume the role of member services supervisor at Riverbend Federal Credit Union, a credit union with 48,000 members and 11 branches in western Michigan.

Last weekend Riverbend moved its accounts to a new computer system. Because of a setup error, about 1,400 automatic loan payments were posted two days late. The new system automatically charged each of those members a $35 late fee, and for some members the late payment also lowered their available credit, so their Riverbend credit cards were declined for several days.

One of those members is the owner of Lakeshore Getaways, a small travel agency that has banked with Riverbend for 14 years. She was leading a group of 20 clients on a tour in Quebec when her company credit card was declined at the hotel front desk in front of the group, and she had to pay with a personal card. She has come into the branch angry. She says she has been “treated like a deadbeat” for the credit union’s mistake, she is thinking about moving both her business and personal accounts to a bank down the street, and she plans to post a review about what happened.

The owner of Lakeshore Getaways (judge) has asked to speak with a supervisor. Your goal is to explain why Riverbend values its relationship with her, show her that you understand the problem from her point of view, calm the situation and recover her trust, and explain what Riverbend’s management is doing so this does not happen to her or other members again.

The owner (judge) will begin the role-play by telling you what happened at the hotel. After you have presented your ideas and answered the owner’s question, the owner will conclude the role-play by thanking you for your time.

Refunding $35 doesn’t fix what happened in front of my clients. What are you actually going to do for me beyond the refund, and how will I know this won’t happen again before my next tour in three weeks?

What you did well

  • You matched the fix to the problem. “We want to avoid seemingly unrelated forms of compensation, like for example, offering her a free dinner” shows real judgment — customers want the actual harm repaired, not a gift.
  • Your remedies were concrete. Refunding the fee, covering what she paid personally, and fixing “the mark we made on her credit” address the money, the inconvenience, and the long-term damage.
  • You covered all four performance indicators in order. Each PI has its own clear section, which makes it easy for a judge to check them off.

What to improve

  • Speak to the judge, not about her. In DECA the judge plays the customer, so every sentence should be said to her. Instead of “starting off by sympathizing with her position,” actually say it: “I’m so sorry. Being declined in front of twenty clients must have been embarrassing, and it was our mistake, not yours.”
  • Use the facts in the case. Mention her 14 years as a member, the tour group, and her next tour in three weeks. Details show the judge you listened. For example: “You’ve trusted us for fourteen years, and we let you down in Quebec.”
  • Answer her threats calmly. She said she may move her accounts and post a review. Acknowledge both without arguing: “I understand why you’d consider that, and I hope what we do today changes your mind.”
  • Make management’s actions specific. “Investigate our systems” is a start; add what the fix is: fees reversed for all 1,400 members, credit limits restored, every member contacted, and the system tested before the next payment cycle.
  • Give proof, not just promises. “The error will be gone” is hard for an angry customer to believe. Offer written confirmation, a direct phone number, or a test charge before her trip.
  • Fix small slips. “If our services our flawed” → are; “As management, is it essential” → it is; “how organize how our customer relations will look like” → how we organize our customer relations.

What the judge was looking for

  1. Positive customer relations: explain that loyal, long-term members are the credit union’s foundation, and say why her 14-year relationship (and her review) matters to Riverbend.
  2. Customer service mindset: show it in your words — apologize, take ownership in the first person, and fix the problem with clear steps and times.
  3. Handling difficult customers: listen, name her feelings (embarrassment, frustration), stay calm, never blame the customer, and respond respectfully to her threats.
  4. Management’s role: describe how management sets service standards and fixes the root cause — reversing every fee, contacting all affected members, and testing the system so it cannot happen again.

Model answer

Thank you for telling me what happened, and I am truly sorry. Having your card declined in front of twenty clients you were responsible for is embarrassing, and it was not your fault. Our new computer system posted your loan payment late, and that one mistake caused the late fee and the lower credit limit. You did nothing wrong, and I want to make this right today.

You have banked with Riverbend for fourteen years, and we value that relationship. A credit union only works when members trust that we will look after them, especially members like you whose business depends on us when you are far from home. That trust is what I want to win back.

Here is what I can do right now. I have already reversed the $35 late fee and removed the late payment from your record, so it will not affect your credit history. I have restored your full credit limit as of this morning, and I will print a letter confirming both. If using your personal card in Quebec cost you any fees or interest, bring me the statement and we will reimburse them. I would also like to give you a letter on Riverbend letterhead that says the decline was our system error, in case any of your clients asked about it.

I also want you to know what management is doing. Our leadership team has found the setup error, and every one of the 1,400 affected members is getting the same fee reversal and a personal call this week. Before the system runs another automatic payment cycle, our operations team is testing it, and we are adding a rule so a late fee cannot be charged when a payment is late because of our own processing. Management sets the standard that our members come first, and that means owning mistakes quickly and fixing the cause, not just the symptom.

I understand you are thinking about moving your accounts and posting a review. That is your choice, and I respect it. I hope that after today you will feel we treated you like the valued member you are.
