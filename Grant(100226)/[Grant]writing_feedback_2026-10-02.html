The topic

Topic: Explain how to stay safe when riding a bike.
Type of writing: Informative — Teach the reader about something, step by step.
How to write it: Start with a sentence that tells the reader what you will explain.Explain the safety steps in order. Use First, Next, Then, and Finally.Add a reason or an example for at least one step (why is it important?).End with a sentence that tells why staying safe matters.
Sentence starter: “Riding a bike is fun, but you need to follow a few rules to stay safe.” (You can use this or write your own first sentence.)
Try to use these words: finally · careful · bright
Task: Write one paragraph (60–120 words). Use the plan above and try to use the three words.

Your writing

Riding a bike is fun, but you need to follow a few rules to stay safe. First, always wear helmet. You should wear a helmet because it can protect your head from hitting the ground when you fall. Next, don’t ride in an area far away without a parent. If you don’t have a parent, you could get lost. So always have a parent with you when you’re riding far away. Then, if you’re first learning how to ride a bike, always use training wheels. Using training wheels can stop you from falling and can get you used to it. Finally, have fun. If you don’t have fun, it is just useless to ride as bike if you don’t enjoy it. So only ride a bike if you enjoy it or if you want to ride a bike. Safety matters because they can help you survive and not get hurt.

What you did great

  • Your steps are in order. You used First, Next, Then, and Finally, just like the plan asked. The reader always knows where they are.
  • You gave a reason, not just a rule. “You should wear a helmet because it can protect your head from hitting the ground when you fall.” That “because” is what makes informative writing teach the reader.

Let’s make it even better

Here is everything that will make your ideas and your paragraph stronger, starting with the most important. (Grammar mistakes are in their own section below.)

  1. 1. Make every step a safety step. Your topic is how to stay safe. “Have fun” is a nice idea, but it does not keep you safe. A better last step is a real safety rule, like wearing bright clothes or watching for cars. You can still say “have fun” in your ending.

    Before: “Finally, have fun.”
    After: “Finally, wear bright clothes and be careful near cars, so drivers can see you.”

  2. 2. Pick steps that help every rider. Training wheels only help kids who are just learning. Most readers already ride without them. A step that helps everyone, like checking your brakes before you ride, teaches more.

    Before: “Then, if you’re first learning how to ride a bike, always use training wheels.”
    After: “Then, check that your brakes and tires work before you ride.”

  3. 3. Say each idea only once. You told the reader to ride with a parent, and then you said it again. When an idea repeats, the reader does not learn anything new. Cut the repeat and use the space for a new detail.

    Before: “So always have a parent with you when you’re riding far away.”
    After: “(Delete this sentence, because the step before already says it.)”

  4. 4. Use only one “if” idea in a sentence. This sentence says “if you don’t” two times, so it goes in a circle. Say the idea once, clearly.

    Before: “If you don’t have fun, it is just useless to ride as bike if you don’t enjoy it.”
    After: “Riding is best when you enjoy it.”

  5. 5. Make your words say exactly what you mean. “If you don’t have a parent” sounds like you have no parents at all! You mean a parent is not riding with you. Also, “it” in “get you used to it” does not tell the reader what “it” is.

    Before: “If you don’t have a parent, you could get lost.”
    After: “If a parent is not with you, you could get lost.”

  6. 6. Make your ending match your topic. “Survive” sounds scary and very serious. Your ending should tell the reader why staying safe matters, in a calm way.

    Before: “Safety matters because they can help you survive and not get hurt.”
    After: “Staying safe matters because it keeps you from getting hurt, so you can keep riding for a long time.”

  7. 7. Keep it closer to 60–120 words. Your paragraph has 150 words. That is a lot of good effort! Cutting the repeated sentences above will bring it into the target range and make it stronger.

    Before: “So only ride a bike if you enjoy it or if you want to ride a bike.”
    After: “(Delete this sentence, because it repeats the one before it.)”

Grammar fixes

  1. 1. Use “a” before a single thing. When you talk about one helmet, put a in front of it.

    Before: “First, always wear helmet.”
    After: “First, always wear a helmet.”

  2. 2. Fix the typo “as bike.” You meant a bike. Read your sentences out loud to catch small slips like this.

    Before: “it is just useless to ride as bike”
    After: “it is just useless to ride a bike”

  3. 3. Make the subject and the word “they” match. “Safety” is one thing, so you cannot call it “they.” Either say “it” or change the subject to “safety rules,” which is more than one thing, and then use matter (not matters).

    Before: “Safety matters because they can help you survive”
    After: “Safety rules matter because they can help you”

Did you use the three words?

  • finally — ✓ Yes! “Finally, have fun.”
  • careful — Not yet. Try: “Be careful when you cross the street.”
  • bright — Not yet. Try: “Wear bright clothes so drivers can see you.”
