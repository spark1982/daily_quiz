The topic

Topic

Explain how to build something with blocks or LEGO.

Type of writing

Informative — Teach the reader about something, step by step.

How to write it

  1. Tell what you are going to build and what pieces you need.
  2. Explain the steps in order: First, Next, Then, Finally.
  3. Add a helpful tip or detail for one step (for example, how to make it strong).
  4. End with a sentence that tells what the reader can do with the finished build.

Sentence starter

“Building a ___ out of LEGO is easy if you follow a few simple steps.” (You can use this or write your own first sentence.)

Your writing

Building a car out of LEGO is easy if you follow a few simple steps. First, you need the pieces. The pieces are wheels, bricks for the shape, a door, pieces that connect wheels to the base, and windows. Next, you put the wheels in the piece that connects the wheels into the base. Then, make the base and put the connector on it. Finally, put the walls and the door and finish it up with the windows. You must be careful or it will look bad. What you did great

  • Your order words make the steps easy to follow. “First, you need the pieces,” “Next,” “Then,” and “Finally” walk the reader through the build one step at a time. That is exactly how informative writers organize a how-to paragraph.
  • You started and ended strong. Your first sentence tells the reader what you will teach, and your last sentence (“make it go up ramps”) shows what they can do with the finished car, just like step 4 of the plan.

Let’s make it even better

Here is everything that will make your paragraph stronger, starting with the most important. (Grammar mistakes are in their own section below.)

  1. 1. Give the “connecting piece” its real name. You wrote “pieces that connect wheels to the base,” “the piece that connects the wheels,” and “the connector.” That’s three different names for one thing, so the reader may think they are three different pieces. The real name is an axle. Say what it is once, then use the name every time.

    Before: “pieces that connect wheels to the base, and windows. Next, you put the wheels in the piece that connects the wheels into the base.”
    After: “windows, and axles, which are the pieces that connect the wheels to the base. Next, you put the wheels on the axles.”

  2. 2. Use the word “piece” less often. Piece or pieces shows up five times. Your list already tells the reader what the pieces are, so you can join the two short sentences with a colon (:) and skip the second “The pieces are.”

    Before: “First, you need the pieces. The pieces are wheels, bricks for the shape, a door…”
    After: “First, you need the pieces: wheels, bricks for the shape, a door…”

  3. 3. Make your helpful tip exact. “You must be careful or it will look bad” is a good idea, but the reader doesn’t know what to be careful about or how it will look bad. Tell them exactly what to do. This is also the perfect spot for the bonus word carefully.

    Before: “You must be careful or it will look bad.”
    After: “Press every brick down carefully, or the car will look crooked.”

  4. 4. Tell the reader what to build the walls with. In your list, the walls come from “bricks for the shape,” but the last step just says “put the walls.” Connect the step to the pieces you listed.

    Before: “Finally, put the walls and the door”
    After: “Finally, build the walls with the bricks, put on the door”

  5. 5. “Strong and sturdy” says the same thing twice. Great job using the bonus word! Sturdy already means strong and hard to break, so you only need one of them.

    Before: “it will be strong and sturdy.”
    After: “it will be sturdy.”

Grammar fixes

  1. 1. We connect something to something. The word connect goes with to, not into. (You got it right the first time: “connect wheels to the base.”)

    Before: “the piece that connects the wheels into the base”
    After: “the pieces that connect the wheels to the base”

  2. 2. “Put” needs to say where. When you put something, the reader wants to know where it goes. Add on (put on the door) or use a more exact verb like build.

    Before: “put the walls and the door”
    After: “build the walls with the bricks, put on the door”

  3. 3. Put a comma before “or” when it joins two full sentences. “You must be careful” and “it will look bad” could each be a sentence. When or, and, or but joins two sentences like that, put a comma before it.

    Before: “You must be careful or it will look bad.”
    After: “You must be careful, or it will look bad.”

  4. 4. We don’t “act” a toy. Act is something a person does (act silly, act in a play). You can’t act it. For a toy, say play with it.

    Before: “you can act it like it is a toy car”
    After: “you can play with it like a toy car”

Every sentence starts with a capital letter and ends with a period, you wrote LEGO correctly, and all your words are spelled right. Nice!

Did you use the three words?

  • first — Yes! “First, you need the pieces.”
  • sturdy — Yes! “it will be strong and sturdy.” (See tip 5 to make it even better.)
  • carefully — Almost! You wrote “careful,” which describes a person. Carefully (with -ly) tells how you do something: “Press every brick down carefully.”

Your writing, polished

Building a car out of LEGO is easy if you follow a few simple steps. First, you need the pieces: wheels, bricks for the shape, a door, windows, and axles, which are the pieces that connect the wheels to the base. Next, you put the wheels on the axles. Then, make the base and put the axles on it. Finally, build the walls with the bricks, put on the door, and finish it up with the windows. Press every brick down carefully, or the car will look crooked. If you do it correctly, it will be sturdy. Then you can play with it like a toy car and make it go up ramps.

The highlighted parts are the changes. Your steps and your ideas stay the same.

Try this next time

When your how-to uses a special piece or tool, give it one name and stick with it. If you don’t know the name, describe it once (“the small rod that holds the wheels”) and then keep calling it the same thing.
