Topic

You wake up and you are the teacher for the day.

Type of writing

Narrative — Tell a story with a beginning, middle, and end.

How to write it

  1. Beginning: tell who you are and how you find out you are the teacher today. Where does the story happen?
  2. Middle: tell what happens in your classroom. What do you teach? What goes right, and what goes wrong?
  3. End: tell how the day ends and how you feel about being the teacher.

Your writing

What you did great

  • You used dialogue to tell the story. “then one student said, “You’re the teacher for the day.”” Letting a character speak is a great way to show a surprise instead of just telling it.
  • You showed your feelings changing. You start out shocked (“I gasped”), then “very nervous”, and at the end you feel “proud of myself”. A story is more exciting when the main character’s feelings change from beginning to end.

Let’s make it even better

Here is everything that will make your story stronger, starting with the most important. (Grammar is in its own section below.)

  1. 1. Tell what you taught and what went wrong. The plan asked, What do you teach? What goes right, and what goes wrong? Your middle says you “started teaching the best I could”, but the reader never finds out what you taught or one thing that happened in class. One small problem makes the middle much more exciting. I added an example — next time, use your own idea.

    Before: “First, I tried to calm down and started teaching the best I could. I was very nervous…”
    After: “First, I took a deep breath to calm down, and then I started teaching a math lesson the best I could. I was very nervous, especially when I mixed up two answers, but the class helped me fix them.”

  2. 2. Make the compliment match the story. In your story you are the teacher, so the student should call you the best teacher, not the best student. Read your dialogue aloud to check that it makes sense.

    Before: ““You’re the best student that has ever taught!””
    After: ““You’re the best teacher we’ve ever had!””

  3. 3. Don’t repeat the same words in one sentence. Your last sentence says “proud of myself” two times. Say it once, then show your feeling in a new way (like smiling).

    Before: “Suddenly I felt proud of myself and when the bell rang, I went back home feeling so proud of myself for teaching the class.”
    After: “Suddenly, I felt proud of myself. When the bell rang, I went home smiling because I had taught the whole class.”

  4. 4. Use a clearer joining word. “I didn’t know why, and then one student said…” uses two joining words where one exact word works better. Until shows that you stopped wondering the moment the student spoke.

    Before: “I didn’t know why, and then one student said…”
    After: “I didn’t know why until one student said…”

  5. 5. Choose exact words. “I didn’t have any experience” is correct, but “I had never taught a class before” is clearer for a reader. Also, you woke up at home and then got to school — “got to” shows the moment you arrived.

    Before: “I didn’t have any experience.” / “went to school”
    After: “I had never taught a class before.” / “got to school”

Grammar fixes

  1. 1. Every part of a sentence needs a verb. “everyone looking at me” is missing a helping verb. Add was so it says what everyone was doing.

    Before: “and everyone looking at me.”
    After: “and everyone was looking at me.”

  2. 2. Put a comma after an opening word like Suddenly. When a sentence starts with a word that tells when or how (Suddenly, Finally, Then), put a comma after it.

    Before: “Suddenly I felt proud”
    After: “Suddenly, I felt proud”

  3. 3. Don’t let a sentence run on too long. Your last sentence joins two complete thoughts with and and no comma. Either put a comma before and or, even better, split it into two sentences.

    Before: “Suddenly I felt proud of myself and when the bell rang, I went back home…”
    After: “Suddenly, I felt proud of myself. When the bell rang, I went home…”

  4. 4. Use an exclamation point for an excited shout. The student is surprising you, so the words are exciting. An exclamation point inside the quotation marks shows that feeling.

    Before: ““You’re the teacher for the day.””
    After: ““You’re the teacher for the day!””

Did you use the three words?

  • nervous — Yes! “I was very nervous…”
  • proud — Yes! “…feeling so proud of myself…” All three words — great job!
