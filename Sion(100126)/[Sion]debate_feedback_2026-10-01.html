The flaw in the argument

Special Pleading — applying a rule or standard to everyone else but making an exception for your own case without a good reason. Here, the opponent said every risky project must show benefits that outweigh its dangers, then said space colonization should not be judged “by the same standards we use for cars and factories.”

Model answer

My opponent sets a clear rule: a project that may cost lives should go forward only if its benefits clearly outweigh its dangers. Then they break that rule. They admit Mars settlements would “probably cost dozens of lives” with radiation risks we would never allow on Earth, yet they say space is “too important” to be judged by ordinary safety standards. That is special pleading: applying a rule to everyone except the case you favor. If a principle is right for factories and cars, it is right for rockets, too. And if the opponent can exempt space just because it feels important, any risky project could claim the same exception.

Applying their own test, the Affirmative loses. They never name a benefit that outweighs dozens of deaths; “the dream of reaching the stars” is a feeling, not a benefit. Meanwhile, we can pursue that dream responsibly. Robotic missions like NASA’s Perseverance rover are exploring Mars right now and sending back real science without risking a single life. Exploration can continue, and safety can improve before humans settle.

So colonizing space now is not a moral imperative. A moral duty cannot require us to abandon our own moral rules. Please vote Negative.
