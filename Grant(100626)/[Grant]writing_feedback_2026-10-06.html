The topic

Topic

Should school start later in the morning?

Type of writing

Opinion — Tell what you think and give reasons.

How to write it

  1. Start by saying what you think: should school start later, or stay the same?
  2. Give two reasons why. Add an example for each reason.
  3. End with a sentence that says your opinion again in a new way.

Sentence starter

“I believe school should ___ because…” (You can use this or write your own first sentence.)

Try to use these words

also  ·  energetic  ·  focused

Task: Write one paragraph (60–120 words). Use the plan above and try to use the three words.

Your writing

I believe school should start later because then you can get more sleep and be more energetic and can participate better in class. What you did great

  • Your opinion is crystal clear. Your first sentence, “I believe school should start later because then you can get more sleep,” tells the reader exactly what you think and why. That is the most important job of an opinion paragraph.
  • You used linking words to add reasons. “I also think” and “Another reason is” help the reader see that a new reason is coming. Your paragraph is 90 words, right in the 60–120 range.

Let’s make it even better

Here is everything that will make your paragraph stronger, starting with the most important. (Grammar mistakes are in their own section below.)

  1. 1. Add an example for each reason. The plan asked you to add an example for each reason. Examples are what make a reader say, “Oh, that makes sense!” Right now your reasons tell, but they do not show.

    Before: “then you can get more sleep and be more energetic and can participate better in class.”
    After: “then students could get more sleep, feel more energetic, and participate better in class. For example, a student who sleeps one extra hour is less likely to yawn all through math class.”

  2. 2. Make each reason different. Your first reason (more sleep makes you energetic) and your last reason (people can be more focused) are almost the same idea. Strong reasons each bring something new. You could keep sleep as one reason and make the second reason about something different, like having time to eat breakfast.

    Before: “Another reason is that the people in class can be more focused and finish their work on time”
    After: “Another reason is that students would have time to eat a good breakfast, so they would stay focused and finish their work on time”

  3. 3. Don’t start every sentence the same way. “School should start later because then” shows up three times. Repeating the same words makes writing sound like a list. Use order words like First, Also, and Finally to move from one reason to the next.

    Before: “I also think school should start later because then for the people”
    After: “Also, starting later would help the people”

  4. 4. Finish with your opinion in a new way. The plan asked for an ending that says your opinion again in a new way. Your last sentence repeats “participate better,” which you already said in your first sentence. Try a fresh sentence that sums up all your reasons.

    Before: “School should start later because then people can participate better and get better grades.”
    After: “Starting school a little later would help students feel rested, stay focused, and earn better grades.”

  5. 5. Use exact words instead of vague ones. “The original time” is a little unclear. Say exactly what you mean so the reader can picture it.

    Before: “than when school starts at the original time”
    After: “than they do now”

Grammar fixes

  1. 1. Fix the mixed-up sentence. After because, a sentence needs a clear subject (who) and a verb (what they do). The word for gets in the way, so the reader cannot tell who will get more rest. Take out “then for” and the sentence works.

    Before: “because then for the people that have to stay up and study can get more rest”
    After: “because the people who have to stay up late studying can get more rest”

  2. 2. Use “who” for people. When you talk about people, use who, not that. Use that for things.

    Before: “the people that have to stay up”
    After: “the people who have to stay up”

  3. 3. Write a list with commas. When you list three things a person can do, put commas between them and use and only once, before the last one. Keep the action words in the same form.

    Before: “get more sleep and be more energetic and can participate better in class”
    After: “get more sleep, feel more energetic, and participate better in class”

  4. 4. Use only one space between words. There are two spaces between “because” and “then.” Always put just one space between words.

    Before: “because  then”
    After: “because then”

Did you use the three words?

  • also — Yes! “I also think school should start later…”
  • energetic — Yes! “…be more energetic…”
  • focused — Yes! “…can be more focused…” You used all three. Awesome!
