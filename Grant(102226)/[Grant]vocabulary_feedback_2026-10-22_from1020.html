conflict (noun) Almost

Your answer

A big fight with someone

Meaning: a disagreement or struggle between people, groups, or ideas

Example: The two friends had a conflict about which game to play first.

You wrote “A big fight with someone.” You are on the right track, but a conflict doesn’t have to be big, and it doesn’t have to be a fight. Two friends who simply disagree have a conflict. In stories, a character can even have a conflict with a storm or with his own fear. Try: “a disagreement or problem between people.”

Your answer

To say something that you think is right

Meaning: what you plan or mean to do

Example: My intention was to finish my homework before dinner.

You wrote “To say something that you think is right.” That sounds more like giving your opinion. An intention is a plan inside your head — what you mean to do. Memory trick: intention starts with intend, and “I intend to” means “I plan to.”

outcome (noun) Almost

Your answer

To wait and see what’s going to happen

Meaning: the result; the way something ends

Example: The outcome of the game was a 3–2 win for our team.

You wrote “To wait and see what’s going to happen.” You’re close — an outcome is what happens in the end. But the word is a thing (a noun), not an action like “to wait.” Think of it as what comes out at the end: the result. Try: “the result of something.”

Words to practice

  • conflict — a disagreement or problem between people (not always a fight)
  • outcome — the result; how something ends
  • intention — what you plan or mean to do

Memory tip: Make a mini-story with all three: “My intention was to share the last cookie, but my sister and I had a conflict. The outcome? We split it in half!”
